Direct your whole video, by eye, before Claude builds it.

Rasa Director is a Claude Code skill for entire videos: launch films, explainers, PR videos, music videos, captioned footage and motion graphics. It shows your own scenes told, styled, moved, cut and scored several ways, you point at what feels right, and every scene is built exactly like that.

HyperFrames lets you pick a look by eye. Nothing lets you pick how things move.

Most people can't describe motion in words, so they never get asked, and the build falls back to the same fade-up-slide on the same power-out ease. Rasa Director adds the missing decision, and makes it by eye.

Every decision, made by eye.

Rasa Director walks you from brief to render through eleven decisions: brief, route, story, scenes, look, motion, transitions, voice, music, storyboard, build. Every one is shown on your own video, in the Director's Console: a local page that opens beside your terminal, where every click goes straight back to Claude.

“You decide” never means the default.

Hand any step back with “you decide”, or everything after it with “you decide the rest”. Claude samples the unusual options instead of the safe one, rotates away from your recent picks, and leaves a receipt: what it chose, and the obvious option it passed over.

Only your content is required. Anything your request already says counts as that step's answer.

  1. 1

    What the video is (a product URL, a topic, a script, a PR, a track, footage), where it plays, how long, voiceover or not. Then which HyperFrames workflow builds it: launch film, explainer, PR video, music video, captions, overlay recut or custom.

    you decide 16:9, the length from your content, the route from what the video is.

    HyperFrames frame presets, chosen on their showcases. Or bring your own brand spec.

    you decide Rotates away from the looks you used last.

  4. 4

    The tasting menu: three of your scenes playing in order, in six personalities side by side, at your real aspect, in your look. One grammar for the whole film. Point at one, or adjust it: “C, but calmer.”

    you decide Tail-weighted, never one of your last three.

  5. 5

    Your first two scenes handing off through every transition, looping. Then your hook line spoken in three voices, or no narration at all.

    you decide The handoff that fits your look, and a voice that fits the story.

  6. 6

    A few music options with players, auditioned in the console, or your own track, or none. The exact track you pick is locked into the final mix.

    you decide One is picked for you, with its receipt.

  7. 7

    The storyboard: the timeline, then every scene sketched in your look. A receipts summary of what you chose and what was decided for you. Then HyperFrames builds every scene with motion.md binding each one, the result is checked, and you render.

Ten ways to move.

Each personality is a complete motion grammar: its own eases, a scale of allowed durations, a stagger, a minimum hold, and a list of banned moves. Ordered by how likely a model is to produce it unprompted, most to least.

The tail

What a model rarely reaches for on its own. Every tasting menu includes two of these, and “you decide” leans this way.

What you picked is what gets built.

Your choice doesn't dissolve into a prompt. It's written into a binding file, handed to every builder, and checked in a real browser before you're asked to render.

videos/tally-launch/motion.mdexcerpt
---
personality: "editorial-mask"
tempo: {"scale_ms":[250,450,700,1000,1400]}
easing: {"enter":"expo.out","exit":"power2.in","move":"expo.inOut"}
stagger: {"each_ms":120}
holds: {"min_ms":1000}
banned: ["fade-up-slide","fade-slide","bounce",
         "overshoot","scale-pop","blur-in"]
runtime: "gsap"
decided_by: "confirmed"
---
# Motion doctrine: Editorial Mask
Lines wipe on like ink across paper, an accent
rule sweeps under the key word. Magazine calm.

  • motion.md

    Your motion, as rules: the eases, the durations allowed, the stagger, how long things hold, and which moves are banned. It travels to every HyperFrames subagent that plans, builds or repairs the piece.

  • The checker

    Headless Chrome loads the built composition, walks every GSAP tween, samples its real start and end values, and compares each one to motion.md. It runs before the render question, never after.

  • Repairs

    Violations go back to HyperFrames' own repair step, at most twice. Anything left is shown to you to fix or waive, one by one. A check that couldn't run is reported as that, never as a pass.

  • Honest previews

    Every personality's own tasting animation passes the checker against its own motion.md with zero errors and zero warnings. The preview you pick from follows the rules the build must follow.

Questions

Does it replace HyperFrames?

No. Rasa Director decides; HyperFrames builds, previews and renders. It hands the matching HyperFrames workflow an approved plan (BRIEF.md, the storyboard and script in its own format, frame.md, motion.md, the music) and checks every scene that comes back.

What does “you decide” actually do?

It hands that step to Claude, and it never means the generic default. Concept and motion are sampled wide with a pull toward the unusual; look rotates away from your recent picks. Each decision comes with a one-line receipt: what was picked, and the obvious option passed over.

Can I skip everything?

Yes. Say “you decide the rest” at any step. Your content is the only thing you have to give. The one question that always runs is “preview first, or render?”

Does it work offline?

Previews and the checker use a vendored copy of GSAP, so they run offline. Fonts come from Google Fonts and fall back to system fonts without a network. HyperFrames itself loads GSAP and fonts from the network when it builds and renders.

What kinds of video?

Launch films, explainers, PR videos, brand reels and custom multi-scene pieces, music and lyric videos, captions for talking-head footage and overlay cards, and short motion graphics like titles and stings. Previews are text-only: scenes are tasted with their words; product shots, logos and data enter at build. Deeper footage editing (cutting graphics into your own clips) is next.

What's the license?

MIT. The vendored GSAP stays under its own GSAP Standard License.
