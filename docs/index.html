Direct the whole video.

Rasa Director is a Claude Code skill for entire videos: launch films, explainers, PR videos, music videos, reels cut from your own footage, motion graphics. You make every creative decision in the terms motion designers use, or hand any of them to Claude. Bring your DESIGN.md and it becomes the look. Then Claude designs and animates every frame to that direction, through HyperFrames, and every scene is checked before you render.

One four-scene film, six motion languages. Swatches to choose by eye; the real film is Claude's.

Every AI video looks the same because nobody made the decisions.

Ask for a launch video and you get the obvious story, a default look, and every line fading up and sliding into place on the same ease. Not because the model can't do better, but because nothing told it what to do instead.

Motion studios don't start with "make it pop". They decide the format, how the product appears, the visual style, the type, the color, the depth, how things move, how scenes hand off and the pacing, and they lock it in style frames before anyone animates. Rasa Director gives you that process and makes Claude follow it.

The vocabulary

A style is a stack of decisions.

Two pieces can both be "2D UI animation" and look nothing alike. Rasa holds the vocabulary of motion design as data: 30 dimensions and 881 terms, each with a definition, what it looks like, how it moves, where it's used, what it's confused with, and the precise instruction Claude follows.

You decide any dimension, or mark it "Claude decides". Claude's picks come from 35 known style combinations that fit what you've already chosen, never the generic default, and each comes with a receipt: what it picked, and the obvious option it passed over.

How it runs

You judge the taste. Claude handles the craft.

A local page, the Director's Console, opens in your browser. It shows what Claude is doing, live, and asks only what is yours to judge, one thing at a time, with Claude's pick already selected. The craft (scenes, pacing, motion, transitions) Claude decides the way a senior motion designer would, and shows you each call in "Your film", where anything can be changed by talking it through.

  1. You decide

    1. Brief what the video is, where it plays, how long, voiceover or not
    2. Brand your DESIGN.md, read back as a board (when you have one)
    3. Style from hundreds of named styles, three picked for you
    4. Story three pitches, each shown as three sketch frames

  2. You hear and see

    1. Voice your hook line in a few voices
    2. Music tracks to audition, or your own
    3. Style frames the key frames, drawn by Claude, approved before anything moves

  3. Claude decides

    1. Workflow which HyperFrames route builds it
    2. Scenes and pacing structure, lengths, reading time
    3. Motion the motion language your style implies
    4. Transitions cuts, match cuts and one signature move

  4. Build

    1. Build Claude animates every scene, the checker verifies it
    2. Render you preview, then render

Your brand

Bring your DESIGN.md.

If your project already has a brand reference, it becomes the look. Its colors and typefaces are used exactly; the directions vary the art direction around them.

  • Reads the common shapes: design.md spec frontmatter with oklch() colors and nested typography, impeccable and gstack files, Stitch-style prose, CSS custom properties, token tables with or without #.
  • Gives every color a role (canvas, ink, accent, surface, muted, status) from its name and comment, then contrast. A saturated "text accent" never becomes the ink; status colors never become the accent.
  • Finds the fonts, skips "banned" ones, maps platform fonts the renderer doesn't have (SF Pro → Inter, SF Mono → JetBrains Mono, Calibri → Carlito) and says so.
  • Writes a frame.md HyperFrames accepts, checks it with HyperFrames' own parser, and stages every font file into the project.

The style library

Hundreds of styles. Pick one by eye.

Every style is a complete look with a proper name: soft neo-brutalism, claymorphism, Swiss grid, Y2K chrome, blueprint HUD, risograph zine and many more. Each is drawn live on your own words, and Claude picks the three that suit your video best, one of them unusual.

Each style carries the motion-design terms that define it (UI treatment, visual style, illustration, stroke, shadow, material, texture, motion language, transitions), so picking one writes a real art-direction brief. With your DESIGN.md, every style is shown in your colors and type. Hover a card to see how it moves.

The motion

A motion language, with a contract.

"If these graphics were alive, what laws would they follow?" Each of the 32 motion languages is a term with a binding contract: its eases, the durations allowed, the stagger, the minimum hold, and the moves it bans. The one you choose becomes motion.md.

Every language's swatch passes the same checker the real build must pass, so what you see is what's enforced. One language governs the whole film; scenes differ only in intensity.

Who animates

Claude designs and animates every frame.

Rasa never ships canned animation. Its boards and swatches exist so you can choose. The frames, the scenes and the cards are Claude's, drawn fresh from your direction.

Before anything moves, Claude designs the key frames as stills for you to approve, the way studios lock art direction before animation. Then it builds every scene through the matching HyperFrames workflow, from DIRECTION.md, frame.md and motion.md.

  1. DirectionDIRECTION.md and motion.md, compiled from your decisions
  2. Style framesClaude draws the key frames; you approve
  3. BuildEvery scene worker reads the direction and the contract in its packet
  4. CardsFor reels, a brief per card; Claude writes each one
  5. CheckEvery scene and card is verified against the contract

You edit it as a list: in and out points, order, cards between clips, overlays on top, captions from what's said, a music bed that ducks under speech. Claude designs and animates every card and overlay from a brief; the build conforms your footage, lints, checks and renders.

The check

What you picked is what gets built.

Your choices don't dissolve into a vague prompt. Every tween in every scene and card is walked in a real browser and compared with the contract before you're asked to render.

  • Durations on the language's scale, eases from its set, written explicitly, never the GSAP default.
  • Banned moves caught in every form, including the generic fade-up-slide.
  • Holds long enough to read; GSAP only, seekable, deterministic.
  • Violations go back to the builder, at most twice; anything left is yours to fix or waive. A check that couldn't run is never reported as a pass.

Install

Needs Claude Code, Node.js 20 or newer, the HyperFrames skills (npx hyperframes skills update), and FFmpeg for footage reels.

Claude Code plugin

From inside Claude Code.

> /plugin marketplace add Sibhimanyu/rasa-director
> /plugin install rasa-director@rasa-director

skills CLI

From your terminal.

$ npx skills add Sibhimanyu/rasa-director

One-line installer

Clones and links the skill.

$ curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Sibhimanyu/rasa-director/master/install.sh | bash

Then try

> /rasa-director make a 45s launch film for https://tally.app
> /rasa-director a product film using our DESIGN.md, like these references: ref1.png ref2.png
> /rasa-director cut ~/Footage/lisbon into a 30s reel with a title card and captions

Check the install with node ~/.claude/skills/rasa-director/scripts/selftest.mjs (52 checks, no network needed).

Questions

Who actually animates the video?

Claude. Rasa Director gives you the decisions and the vocabulary; its boards and swatches are only for choosing by eye. Claude designs the style frames, the scenes and the reel cards from your direction, HyperFrames renders them, and a checker confirms the motion follows the contract you chose.

Do I need to know motion-design terms?

No. Every term comes with a plain definition, what it looks like and what it's used for, and anything you skip, Claude decides. The terms are what make the result precise: "simplified UI, grotesk, precise motion, UI-morph transitions" builds something very different from "make it modern".

Can it use my brand?

Yes. Put your DESIGN.md (or design.md) in the project. Rasa reads it, shows you what it understood as a brand board, and uses your colors and fonts exactly, either as the look or across new design directions.

What does “you decide” actually do?

It hands that decision to Claude, and it never means the generic default: candidates come from known style combinations that fit your other choices, the generic option is excluded, recent picks rotate out, and every choice comes with a receipt naming the obvious option passed over.

Can I skip everything?

Yes. Say “you decide the rest” at any step; only the source (what the video is about) is required. The one question that always runs is “preview first, or render?”

Does anything leave my machine?

The console runs on 127.0.0.1 with a per-session token and serves files only from your workspace. Site capture, voice samples, music and fonts go through HyperFrames' tools, Google Fonts or Fontshare; nothing else leaves your machine.
