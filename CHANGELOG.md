# Changelog

## 1.3.0 (2026-10-01)

- **A crew of agents** (`references/crew.md`, briefs in `skills/rasanai/agents/`). Claude is the Director, and twelve roles do the work that one context did badly: a **research desk** (product, brand, screens, precedent films, and a local scout), a **research lead** who merges it into one truth sheet and a claims ledger, a **writers' room** (three writers in parallel, one per story device, and a hostile script editor), a **Motion Director** who scores the whole film (spine, motif, energy curve, every shot, every seam with handoff numbers on both sides, one signature) and later checks every cut, **frame designers** for the key frames, one **scene animator** per scene working in parallel, and **critics** with fresh eyes (frames, motion, grounding, film). Members never talk to the user. Each starts from a prompt file the Director writes (`crew.mjs brief`) and is accepted only on `crew.mjs check`.
- **Research before story; nothing from memory.** What a product does, says and looks like today now comes from sourced research: features and releases with dates, the exact UI words, real numbers, users' own words, what's no longer true, the real logo, colours, type and the brand's own motion signature (read from its CSS), real screens with a measured UI kit, and, for public brands, its past launch films measured shot by shot (`research.mjs film`: every cut, shot lengths, pacing, a contact sheet; the download is deleted after). The test case: a ChatGPT launch film made from one logged-out capture put grey placeholder bars where answers should be, set the film in Inter, and showed a Plugins menu that no longer exists.
- **Your own projects, with permission.** `research.mjs local-find` looks for the product's code and earlier videos on this computer (folder names, package names, git remotes and README titles only). The console asks before anything is read, and again before running the app. The scout then reads only the approved folders: UI strings, design tokens, the changelog and recent commits, fixtures, logos and screenshots. It never copies secrets.
- **The score is the visual design.** `crew.mjs storyboard` writes the Motion Director's score into the project's STORYBOARD.md in the HyperFrames workflow's own format: `## Video direction`, and for every frame its shot sequence, blueprint, focal, roles, sfx, `handoff_in` / `handoff_out` and `transition_in`. It is checked with the workflow's parser, so the parallel frame packets carry each scene's part of one plan. `crew.mjs check` refuses a score with a missing seam, a half-stated handoff, a changed duration, a flat energy curve, one entrance type on more than 30% of entrances, three scenes in a row with the same layout, or two crash zooms.
- **Seeing motion.** `crew.mjs strip` renders a composition, an assembled project or a video at chosen times (or at 15 fps across a move) into a labelled contact strip. Animators look at their own motion before handing it back; the Motion Director and the critics judge seams and eases from frames, not code.
- **The crew is pushed to show off.** Claude does its best motion work when it's told to show off, so the push is built in rather than left to the user. Every creative brief says it, and every creative prompt ends on it. The Motion Director must name 2 to 4 showreel moments, which reach every animator through the storyboard. Each animator reports the moment it's proudest of. The critics score ambition and fail competent-but-safe work. Showing off means craft (choreography, invisible seams, one spectacle beat), never more effects: the slop gates still apply.
- The console's brief shows **What I found** (the research's most useful facts, each with its source), and the Decisions drawer has a **Research** entry.
- Self-test section 14 covers the crew: the plan, the prompt files, the score's checks, the storyboard write (idempotent, parsed), local search and inventory (no secrets) and the motion strip.

- **The source is asked in the console, never in chat.** With no URL, topic or footage in the request, the console shows "What's the video about?" (a fresh console shows it before Claude's first push, and the brief shows it when the subject is empty).
- **Console fixes from a design review.** Toasts were invisible (white on light grey) and sat on the Tell Claude bar; text on the yellow accent and the green badges was white; Browse all styles didn't dim the page; the Decisions drawer had no elevation and content scrolled under the bottom bar; Decisions credited every call to Claude, even the brief you submitted; and Claude's questions appeared under the animatic, below the fold. ## 1.0.0 (2026-10-01)

Rasa Director 1.0 rebuilds the product around one idea: when thirty people make an AI video, they get thirty videos with different content and the same film. Every default that makes that happen (the obvious story, the default look, one ease for everything, a looping music bed, a whoosh on every cut, glowing text) is now a decision, and the build is checked against it.

- **Four calls, and you see your film at every one.** The console asks four things: the **Brief** (one editable sentence: "A 45-second launch film for Tally, 16:9 for the website, with voiceover, in Tally's own colours and type"), the **Films** (three complete films, each a named design system drawn live and moving on the film's own opening line, with a story angle, Sure · Bold · Wild, a logline and its music), the **Animatic** (every scene's key frame at its real length with the real music edit and the voice; click any moment to leave a note, then watch it build scene by scene) and the **Final** (the render with scene markers, notes, versions and what changed). Claude decides everything else and lists each call with its reason in a **Decisions** drawer; ⌘K "Tell Claude" changes anything. Claude recommends the most ambitious film that still tells the truth, not the safe one.
- **A new console.** A dark, neutral review room where the film is the brightest thing on screen: one editable brief sentence, live film tiles, an animatic player with pinned notes, a build strip that fills in, a final player with markers and versions, and keyboard shortcuts (Space, C for a note at the playhead, 1·2·3, ⌘K). Notes collect until you press Apply; Claude makes them in one pass and marks them resolved (`console.mjs resolve`).
- **The story engine** (`story.mjs`, `taxonomy/devices.json`, `references/story.md`): a truth sheet written from the product (what really changes, for whom, in its own words and numbers), 66 narrative devices from great launch films and ads, three pitches that differ on at least five of seven axes, and a rubric that rejects the default arc and any pitch that would work for any product. Story devices rotate across your films.
- **403 design systems in 20 families.** Eight new families (heritage, broadcast, science, print, interface, nature & material, music & scene, place & wayfinding) join the original twelve. Every style is a taxonomy stack, a recipe the console draws live, a motion contract and a complete DESIGN.md (`design-system.mjs export`, `export-all`; all 403 are on the website to download, and every one reads back exactly as a brand).
- **A far more expressive style renderer**: new layouts (map, split, big number, type grid, timeline, photo, stack, list, isometric, chat, ticker, floor plan, record sleeve), effects (RGB split, extrusion, halation, grain, vignette, depth blur, glint, scanlines, misregistration, light leak), textures (hatching, checker, wood grain, stone veining, weave, terrazzo, perforation), pattern motifs (tiles, stripes, repeats, pictograms), photo scenes and colour ramps, chart types, per-style labels and icon sets. Decorative corner coordinates and fixed filler words are gone.
- **Music edited to picture** (`sound.mjs`, `music.mjs`, `references/sound.md`): tracks analysed with nothing but Node and FFmpeg (tempo, beats, bars, sections, a real ending, loudness); an edit plan that never loops audibly (played into the track's own ending, one bar-line jump, or a designed button, with the film flexing up to a second), scene starts snapped to bars, the reveal on a section start and the logo on the final hit; beds mastered to −14 LUFS; candidates requested long enough for the film, with licences recorded; `needs_longer` instead of a loop.
- **Sound design with restraint**: `sound.mjs sfx-plan` places effects only on visible, causal events, within a density budget, never where the music already hits, each at its measured sync point; `sound.mjs check` measures loudness, true peak, quiet openings, abrupt endings, audible loops, music under the voice and SFX density.
- **The anti-slop gate** (`slop.mjs`): copy ("seamless", "unlock", "not X, it's Y", em dashes, "Introducing…"), look (neon glow text, purple-blue gradients, particle filler, lens flares, corner timecodes, centred everything, Inter everywhere), motion (idle loops, spinning logos, every element entering the same way), timing (unreadable text, uniform shot lengths, no end hold, a slow hook) and sound (a bed shorter than the film, SFX overload, a whoosh on more than one cut in three), plus the rendered video (dead air, loudness, a black open). It runs on the key frames before you see them and on the build before the render.
- **Craft playbook v2** (`references/craft.md`): precise, quantified rules for everything Claude decides (hooks, reading time, rhythm, motion, transitions, composition, camera, lighting, sound, story, anti-slop, quality gates, how decisions are reported), and **a film vocabulary** (`references/vocabulary.md`): about 230 terms (camera, optics, lighting, grade, editing, animation, texture, type, sound), each with an HTML/CSS/GSAP recipe with numbers. Two new taxonomy dimensions, **lighting** and **colour grade** (32 dimensions, 926 terms).
- **Key frames guide the build**: the approved animatic frames travel into the project (`assets/keyframes/`) and every frame worker builds toward its scene's frame.
- `audio-lock` recognises a mastered bed and sets its level under the voice; Roboto is no longer mistaken for a platform font when reading a DESIGN.md.

## 0.4.3 (2026-09-30)

- **Works without Node installed**: setup is now one command, `bash scripts/setup.sh --open`, which needs only bash and curl. ## 0.4.0 (2026-09-30)

- **The taxonomy**: 30 dimensions and about 880 terms (format, UI treatment, product-demo language, UI interaction, data viz, photo/video, illustration, character, iconography, visual style, era, color, typography, composition, shape, stroke, shadow, material, texture, depth, motion language, motion function, transitions, camera, effects, pacing, narrative, sound, production, tone), each with a definition, looks, motion, uses, what it conveys, what it's confused with, search terms and a precise prompt; 35 style combinations; a schema and validator (`taxonomy.mjs`).

## 0.3.0 (2026-09-30)

**Footage reels.** Point Rasa at a folder of raw clips and it cuts them into a reel or edit, with motion-graphics cards between clips and titles, lower thirds and captions on top.

- `reel.mjs scan` examines every clip: length, size after phone rotation, fps, sound, a contact sheet and poster, shot changes, silences, and a word-level transcript.
- `reel.mjs build` turns the cut (reel.json) into a HyperFrames composition: each used segment conformed to the reel's frame (cover with focus, letterbox, or blur-fill) at a constant frame rate, cards and overlays drawn in the chosen look and motion and sized to their requested length, clip transitions in the motion's own handoff (cut, dissolve, wipe), captions remapped through the cut, and a music bed that ducks under speech. It stages the look's fonts, runs `hyperframes lint` and `obey.mjs`, and renders.
- Console: **Footage** (the clips, their contact sheets and transcripts) and **Cut** (the editable edit: in/out points, order, cards, overlays, captions, a draft to watch).
- The landing page's tasting demos play three scenes; its copy covers whole videos and reels.
- Self-test: 39 checks (CI installs FFmpeg).
