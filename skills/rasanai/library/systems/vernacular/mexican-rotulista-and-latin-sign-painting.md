## What it is

A rotulo is a hand-painted sign on a shop, stall, wall or shutter; the craftspeople are rotulistas, and the practice is called rotulismo. It is common across Latin America and Mexico, still "viable forms of commercial art in working and middle-class neighborhoods" (Wikipedia). Sources trace its popularity to the years after the Mexican Revolution (Remezcla; Materia says the profession grew with the boom of small businesses), and describe it as "el otro muralismo", the other muralism, with Orozco, Rivera and Siqueiros as its relatives at a smaller commercial scale (It's Nice That). Kurt Hollander describes rotulos as a continuation of pre-Hispanic glyph traditions and revolutionary graphic art (Designboom). Materia documents the training line: Isaias Salgado Del Pilar, a master rotulista with over 30 years' experience, learned at 17 in his uncle's workshop in Peralvillo, Mexico City. By the 1980s Mexico City had many workshops; vinyl and digital printing reduced them by the 2000s (Materia).

## The rules that make it this and not something else

- The sign is painted by one person for one business; the character of the hand is the product (It's Nice That: "ingenious colour, character and humour").
- Lettering is built, not set. A small repertoire of alphabets, learned from a common manual (the Speedball manual in a Mexican edition of 1972 listed by Materia: helvetica, brush, gothic, old english), is adapted by each painter to the board.
- Every sign pairs words with an image or a joke; typical content is "smart one-liners or literal directions" on storefronts and windows, and slogans on highway walls (Materia).
- Colour is bright and used as an invitation: "bright colors and humor as an enticement" (Materia).
- Texts fit boards: line lengths are matched by scaling letters.
- The look has been marked by class: Hollander points to the term "naco", once a slur for indigenous people in cities and later a word for vibrant working-class visual expression, and says upper and middle classes dismissed the style as cheap and flashy.
- The loss is political as well as aesthetic: Hollander calls the 2022 Cuauhtemoc ban on hand-painted stall signs "a racist and classist erasure" (as quoted in the Designboom article; his stated view).
- It continues in new hands: Alfonso Gonzalez Jr. of Los Angeles works with his father Alfonso Gonzalez Sr., a sign painter for over 40 years, "to not lose that tradition but make it feel like it was made today" (Remezcla).
- Not covered here: Peruvian, Colombian and other regions' sign painting and bus art; none of those was fetched this session, so this entry is Mexican-first by the brief and does not describe them.

## Tokens decoded

- Ground: flat enamel colour on the wall or board; if a canvas is needed use #f4e9cf (proposed) or one saturated colour field.
- Colours (proposed): red #e0301e, yellow #f6c218, blue #1c5db3, green #139b6a, black #14110f, white. Use a three-colour sign with one accent and one shadow colour.
- Type: choose from two of Kaushan Script (brush), UnifrakturCook (old English), Oswald (gothic block). All three are Google Fonts families (title confirmed this session).
- Outline 4 px, drop shadow 8 px lower-right in a darker hue of the letter colour, a second white inline allowed on big letters.
- Image zone: simplified, 60 percent of board height; a food, animal or tool painted in three tones with highlights drawn as white strokes.
- Texture: brush ridges (a 1-2 px noise displacement along stroke direction) and a few light drips, no wear unless the story calls for it.

## Motion and camera

2D
- Paint on: a mask wipes each word left to right at brush speed: 420 ms per word on `power2.out`, 80 ms between words.
- Fill, then outline: the outline appears 120 ms after the fill, then the shadow slides in 8 px over 160 ms (`power2.out`).
- The image zone is painted by 3-5 mask strokes of 200 ms each, large to small.
- The joke line arrives last, 280 ms after the sign finishes, in a smaller alphabet.
- Hold 900-1200 ms; a street of signs is a lateral 1200 px truck over 3.2 s on `sine.inOut`, each sign on a depth factor of 0.8-1.2 to break the flatness.
- Cuts on a beat to a close-up of the brush stroke, a detail: the ridge of a letter, the edge of a shadow.

3D reading. A street as layered planes: shutters at z = 0, awnings 0.3 m in front, a stall sign 0.8 m, a hanging sign 1.5 m; `k.pxPlane` for each painted board with its texture, `k.material("matte")`, a late-afternoon `k.rig("window")` with `dir` [-1, 0.5, 0.7], lens 35 mm trucking sideways 3 m over 4 s (`sine.inOut`), `fstop` 4 so the near signs soften, a 0.4 s hold on one sign before the cut. No rotating signboard in a void, no neon.

## How to instruct a model to build it

> HyperFrames, 1920x1080, 30 fps, GSAP, one paused timeline. Build a Mexican rotulista sign scene. A flat painted ground in one saturated enamel colour, a board with a 3 px inner frame. Lettering is painted: two alphabets only (brush: Kaushan Script; gothic: Oswald), each letter with a 4 px outline and an 8 px hard drop shadow in a darker hue, lower-right; scale each line to fill the board width. Palette from [#e0301e, #f6c218, #1c5db3, #139b6a, #14110f] (proposed). An image zone (food or an animal) in three tones plus white highlight strokes. Sequence: each word is painted on with a left-to-right mask, 420 ms `power2.out`, words 80 ms apart; outline 120 ms after fill; shadow slides 8 px in 160 ms; the image zone in 3-5 strokes of 200 ms; a one-line joke arrives 280 ms after; hold 1000 ms. Add a 1-2 px brush-ridge displacement to the letters. No smooth vector gradient, no faux-wobble, no pastel desert palette. Treat the sign as the voice of a working business, credit the craft, and avoid the word 'quaint'. Claude: plan the line lengths so lettering fills the board. GPT: specify the exact outline and shadow offsets or it will use a default drop shadow blur.

## Blending notes

- Carries: built lettering, outline-plus-shadow convention, board-filling, jokes, painted-on motion.
- With Ghanaian posters or truck art: both are working-painter traditions of the people's own streets; keep colour density and hand evidence, avoid collapsing them into a single 'global folk' look.
- With Swiss: use rotulo style as an interlude; keep Swiss for data.
- Breaks: thin type, airy white space, slick gradients.
