## What it is

The Cuban Institute of Art and Film Industry (ICAIC) was founded in March 1959, 83 days after the Revolution, and its poster department made posters part of the work of a national cinema, not only advertising (Georgetown Library). The designers combined photography, Abstract Expressionism, pop and Art Nouveau ideas (Georgetown). Domestic production was mostly silkscreen, because lithographic equipment was scarce, and silkscreen allowed quantities without degradation (cuba50.org); OSPAAAL's solidarity posters, circulated worldwide with the Tricontinental, used offset lithography. The best-known ICAIC designers were Eduardo Munoz Bachs, Alfredo Rostgaard, Raul Martinez, Antonio Fernandez Reboiro and Rene Azcuy Cardenas. Each had a distinct hand, and the shared quality is a single strong idea per sheet.

## The rules that make it this and not something else

- Designs focus on "the essence or central character of a particular film" (Georgetown), not a cast montage.
- Flat inks, few of them, overprinted; the screen process is visible in the result.
- Wit as a method. Rostgaard's work is "playful, balancing serious political subjects with visual wit" (Wikipedia); Munoz Bachs's style is flat colours, humour and simplified, childlike figures with hand lettering (Wikipedia and the search summary).
- One designer, one film, one decision: the poster is an idea and not a template. Munoz Bachs made over 2,000 posters for ICAIC from 1960 (Wikipedia, Georgetown), among them Soy Cuba (1964), Vampiros en La Habana (1985) and Fidel aprieta que a Cuba se respeta (1980); Georgetown credits the iconic Soy Cuba poster to Rene Portocarrero, so attribute carefully when you cite it.
- Antonio Fernandez Reboiro's Harakiri (1964) won a prize in an international film poster competition (Georgetown); his Moby Dick (1968) is on the Puppies and Flowers page.
- Influences are stated plainly: Art Nouveau (Julio Eloy Mesa's La bella del Alhambra, 1989, after Mucha), pop and psychedelic (Rostgaard), pop portraiture (Martinez, per cuba50).
- Rostgaard's timeline (Wikipedia): art director of Mella magazine 1963; Havana and ICAIC posters from 1965; art director of OSPAAAL 1966-1975. His NOW! (1965) appears on the Puppies and Flowers page.
- Poster is an artwork in a cultural policy: cuba50 describes posters that "engage the viewer as a thinking person" and that appear in a golden age by 1965; about five million posters were printed in 1972 alone (cuba50), covering education, health and politics as well as film.

## Tokens decoded

- Stock: cream or tinted paper #f1e9d6 (proposed), full bleed or a thin margin.
- Inks (proposed): black #171717, red #e4372b, blue #1a4fa0, yellow #f2c230, green #2a9d6f. Limit a poster to 3-4 inks; let overprint produce the extra colour (multiply blend).
- Type: Londrina Solid for rounded hand-lettered titles, Archivo Narrow for credits (both confirmed Google Fonts by title).
- Shapes: large, simplified, unmodulated; hair-thin detail only in one ink.
- Registration: offset one ink by 1-2 px against the key for the print look.
- Image rule: one figure or object plus one twist (a cut, a substitution, a visual pun).

## Motion and camera

2D
- Build the poster ink by ink: each ink layer is a full-frame flat shape sliding in 40-80 px on `power3.out`, 300 ms, 140 ms stagger; where two inks cross, use `mix-blend-mode: multiply` so the third colour appears as they land.
- The gag: after the last ink lands, a single hard transform (a shape swapping, a hat becoming a moon, a letter dropping) in 160 ms on `power2.inOut`; hold 900 ms.
- Title: drops 30 px with a 2-frame bounce (`back.out(1.4)`, 320 ms).
- Cut to the next poster with a 4-frame colour flash in the poster's accent ink; no dissolves.
- Camera: orthographic locked; a 4 percent push over the hold. No parallax and no 3D tilt.

3D reading. Silkscreen layers as planes: for each ink a `k.pxPlane` with a flat colour or texture, 0.01-0.03 m apart, `k.material("paper")` so they take a flat key, orthographic-feeling lens 135 mm so the plane stack stays graphic; `k.rig("top-soft")` with `dir` [0, 0.5, 1]; the camera arcs 12 degrees over 2.4 s `power3.inOut` to reveal the registration offsets between inks, then returns flat for a hold (the `depth-to-flat` seam). No glossy sheen, no ink gradients.

## How to instruct a model to build it

> HyperFrames, 1920x1080, 30 fps, GSAP, one paused timeline. Build a Cuban ICAIC-style silkscreen poster scene. One cream stock layer (#f1e9d6, proposed). Draw the poster as 3-4 full-frame flat ink layers (black #171717, red #e4372b, blue #1a4fa0, yellow #f2c230, all proposed), inline SVG, no gradients, no blur, one idea only: the central figure of the film plus one visual pun. Colours overlap with `mix-blend-mode: multiply`. Offset the blue layer 1.5 px from the key. Lettering is hand-drawn rounded type (Londrina Solid) as part of the picture; credits in Archivo Narrow, small. Sequence: each ink slides in 60 px on `power3.out` in 300 ms at 140 ms stagger; at 1.1 s a single hard morph (160 ms `power2.inOut`) delivers the joke; title bounce 320 ms `back.out(1.4)`; hold 900 ms; hard cut with a 4-frame flash in the accent ink. Do not use a Che-style star, photo halftone, glow or drop shadow. Claude: state the one idea and the pun before drawing. GPT: give the exact ink list and forbid extra colours.

## Blending notes

- Carries: ink-by-ink build, overprint multiply, one-idea sheets, hand lettering, wit.
- With Polish poster school or Push Pin: strong match through conceptual illustration.
- With data styles: use the poster only for the headline beat; bars as flat inks.
- Breaks: photographic detail, realistic lighting, strictly rational grids.
