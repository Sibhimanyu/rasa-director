## What it is
Hand-painted signs and letters on tin sheets, plywood boards, cinema hoardings and vehicles were once ubiquitous in Indian cities. The painters developed "hyperlocal typographic dialects" in Devanagari, Bengali, Tamil, Gujarati, Gurmukhi and Urdu (Homegrown), and V.S. Naipaul is quoted there as noticing that sign painters had a feeling for both Roman and Devanagari letters. Sahapedia's article on Delhi names painters (Kafeel near Jama Masjid, Painter Shakeel for Urdu, Charan Painter) and lists the places signs lived: Chandni Chowk, Jama Masjid, Paharganj, Connaught Place, Shankar Market, Gole Market. Signs were made on site, taking account of light, neighbouring signs and the comments of people passing, an "interactive" practice.

The craft is being documented by people who work with the painters. The Handpainted Type, founded in 2011 by Hanif Kureshi (St+Art co-founder, died September 2024), digitised painters' lettering into downloadable fonts with royalties going to the painters, and brought painters such as Kafeel new work. Pooja Saxena's India Street Lettering records scripts, locations, functions and techniques, and she began Type Walks in Delhi in 2017; Tanya George has run Type Walks in Mumbai since 2018; Aashim Tyagi's Street Type Archive has run for over 15 years. The practice has been in decline: tin boards gave way to acrylic in the 1980s; silk-screen, vinyl cutters and large-format printers arrived in the 1990s; free standard boards from large brands, Delhi municipal by-laws of 1993 that treated old signs as visual pollution, and a 2010 Commonwealth Games clean-up (all Sahapedia) pushed them out. In Chennai flex did the same to banners (DT Next).

## The rules that make it this and not something else
- **Each script has its own construction.** Devanagari hangs from a continuous headline bar, Tamil letters have attached vowel signs and round forms, Urdu in Nastaliq rides a diagonal baseline. A sign is lettered by someone who reads the script.
- **Fit the board.** Lines are stretched, squeezed or tilted to fill the width; the shop name is biggest. Sizes follow importance, not a type scale.
- **Three-dimensional lettering.** Sahapedia records letters with shadows and layered paint, bevelled prismatic lettering of geometric construction, and perspective distortion and sculptural effects. Richard Frazer describes 1980s-graffiti-like drop shadows, reflections, shines, fat outlines and gradient fills, and crowns over capitals.
- **Seven-colour (satrangi) style** for juice stalls: many colours in one board, each letter its own colour (Sahapedia).
- **Cinema influence.** Cinema poster title design from the 1950s to the 1980s fed commercial signage (Sahapedia): the outlined, shadowed title is shared with **bollywood-hand-painted** and **tamil-cinema-posters**.
- **Regional difference.** Kerala has ultra-bold slab-serif lettering on carved name boards in saturated colour (Frazer). Mumbai combines Art Deco lettering, tile letterforms, wooden signs and cinema signage (It's Nice That).
- **Multi-script boards.** Latin, Devanagari, Nastaliq, Gurmukhi and more occur on one board, each in its own band.
- **Names lost.** Homegrown and Sahapedia say many sign painters' names have been lost; credit those who are named, and say so when the painter is unknown.

## Tokens decoded
- Canvas: a flat ground in one strong colour; letters in white or yellow with a black outline; shadow in a second hue. Hexes proposed (not sourced).
- Type: build lines from real fonts only when needed, with per-line horizontal scale: `transform: scaleX(...)` so each line fills the board width, plus `text-shadow` stacks for a bevel (8 layers, 2 px steps, darker each step) and `-webkit-text-stroke: 4px`. Yatra One 200 px for Devanagari; Tiro Tamil or Baloo Thambi 2 for Tamil; keep `lang` attributes set; never letterspace Indic scripts.
- Grid: board 1600 x 640 px centred in 1920 x 1080 with a 24 px painted border, three bands (60 / 25 / 15 percent), 2 px chalk guideline.
- Shape: ribbons, scrolls and crowns as simple SVG; products painted flat with an outline.
- Texture: wall plaster tile 512 px at 8 percent, tin sheen as a 6 percent top-to-bottom gradient overlay, 1 px edge wobble via `feTurbulence` (baseFrequency 0.04, scale 3).

## Motion and camera
2D:
- **Sign-painter's sequence (signature).** 0 ms chalk baseline and cap line draw (stroke-dashoffset, 320 ms `power2.out`). 300 ms outline path draws per letter (letters stagger 50 ms, each 220 ms `power2.out`). 900 ms fill floods (clip from left, 400 ms `power3.out`). 1300 ms shadow extrudes by 14 px (240 ms `power2.out`). Hold 1.2 s.
- **Satrangi.** Each letter takes the next of seven colours in sequence, colour changes on 90 ms steps with `steps(1)`-like behaviour via separate timeline sets; no continuous gradient.
- **Lane walk.** A row of boards at depths 0.4, 0.8, 1.0, 1.6 with lateral truck 12 percent over 5 s `power1.inOut` and a 600 ms rest at the chosen board; the shop's board lights (brightness +20 percent, 200 ms) as the camera arrives.
- **Urdu direction.** Entrances run right to left, Devanagari and Latin left to right; mixed boards stagger by script band.

3D reading (Rasan3D):
- Build the street from `k.pxPlane` boards at z -2.0, -1.0, 0 (the hero board), 1.2 (a foreground rope or a bicycle plane out of focus); walls with `k.material("concrete", { color })`; boards `k.material("matte")` with the painted texture.
- Lit shop board only: `k.extrudeText` in a TTF with `depth` 0.02, bevel 0.004, and a thin `emissive` strip at intensity 2 for a tube light; Devanagari or Tamil only if the TTF has the glyphs (`k.extrudeText` expects TTF/OTF, not WOFF2), and shaping of complex scripts in extruded geometry is not assured: use `k.text`/`k.image` of the painted board for complex scripts.
- Rig `low-key` with a sodium-warm key `dir: [-0.4, 0.2, 0.9]`; lens 35 mm, f/2.8; a truck of 1.2 m over 4 s (`power3.inOut`); a rest of 0.8 s on the hero board.

## Blending notes
- Carries: fit-to-board lettering, bevel plus offset shadow, script bands, painted guide lines, board scale.
- Pairs with **indian-truck-art**, **bollywood-hand-painted**, **tamil-cinema-posters** (lettering), **indian-matchbox-and-calendar-art** (banners).
- Breaks it: a single tidy typeface, large empty margins, even stroke weights, invented text.
