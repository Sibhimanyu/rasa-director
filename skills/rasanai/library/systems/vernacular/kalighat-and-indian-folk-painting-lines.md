## What it is
Kalighat painting arose in 19th-century Calcutta around the Kalighat Kali temple, where patuas (scroll painters) from rural Bengal made quick pictures for pilgrims and visitors. The V&A holds about 645 works (mostly A3, with 295 postcard-sized pieces of roughly 13 x 8 cm), dated 1830s to 1930s, including John Lockwood Kipling's 233 donations of 1917. They are mainly watercolour on mill-made paper bought ready, with some line drawings and hand-coloured lithographs, "characterised by bright colours and bold outlines" on plain backgrounds with minimal detail, simplified to allow rapid production (V&A). The move from long narrative scrolls to a single portable frame was a market decision: faster, cheaper, urban.

Production was a workshop line: one family member pencilled the outline, another modelled, another coloured, and the outlines were last, in lamp black (Wikipedia, quoting a painter). Penn Museum describes quick-drying watercolour replacing gouache and tempera, and a wash graded from dark at the form's edge to bare paper in the middle, an adaptation of European volume modelling. The subjects widened from gods to the city: the babu, the courtesan, the 1873 Tarakeshwar murder case. Goddesses wear Victorian crowns and play violins (Penn). Ajit Ghose in 1926 compared the brush line to Chinese calligraphy for its "boldness and vigour". Cheap German chromolithographs ended the market by the early 20th century.

## The rules that make it this and not something else
- **One figure, one frame, plain ground.** The ground is the paper itself; the figure is large and a little low.
- **The line is the signature.** One sweeping contour that swells and tapers. Line weight variation matters more than any ornament.
- **Volume by wash.** Colour darkest at the periphery, bare paper in the middle. No cast shadow, no hatching, no gradient mesh.
- **Thin, bright, flat pigment.** 3 to 4 colours; silver paint for jewellery.
- **Content looks at its own city.** A deity or a clerk; the satire is in pose and props. Gaze often not at the viewer.
- **Not a scroll, not a fill pattern.**

### How it differs from related Indian folk lines (do not blend by accident)
- **Patachitra (Bengal scroll):** the ancestor, long narrative scrolls; Kalighat dropped the scroll for a frame (V&A, Wikipedia).
- **Madhubani (Mithila):** walls and floors, now paper; tools are fingers, twigs, nib-pens, matchsticks; "no space is left empty"; five styles (Bharni, Kachni, Tantrik, Godna, Kohbar) (Wikipedia). Opposite of Kalighat's empty ground.
- **Warli (Maharashtra):** white rice-flour paste on red-brown mud walls, a chewed bamboo stick, circle / triangle / square, the chauk (Wikipedia). Pictogram geometry, no modelled figure.
- **Jamini Roy:** learned from Kalighat, turned to rural subjects, lacks its "grim sense of humour" (Wikipedia). A later modernist reading.

## Tokens decoded
- Canvas #efe4d1 (measured from the V&A montage, paper cluster 25.7%). Keep 20 to 30 percent of the frame empty around the figure.
- Ink #1b201c as a filled path with a width profile, 6 to 14 px at 1080p; do not rely on `stroke-width`.
- Colour: rust #b54e1d, turmeric #d9ab53, sage #717a63 (measured), indigo #25407a (proposed); each with a darker wash at the edge (multiply the colour by about 0.7 at the rim; proposed).
- Type: none or a caption. Hind Siliguri 28 to 36 px below the figure; Tiro Bangla for a single Bengali title line.
- Shape: no cards or containers; the page is the frame.
- Content: invented subjects in this construction; satire of a contemporary type is faithful to the tradition.

## Motion and camera
2D (vocabulary: trim paths / draw-on, mask reveal, hold, moving hold):
- **The sweep.** Contour as a masked path, `strokeDashoffset` length to 0, 700 ms `power2.inOut`; inner details +200 ms at 400 ms `power2.out`. Butt caps, or gate opacity so no dot shows at 0. Two or three overlapping sub-paths with different widths read as one swelling line.
- **Flood.** Flat colour fills fade up in 180 ms `power1.out`, stagger 60 ms, torso, face, props. No slide, no scale.
- **Wash.** An edge-darkening layer (radial mask) 0 to 1 in 360 ms `power2.out`.
- **Rest.** Hold the finished page at least 1.2 s.
- **Camera.** Locked. Moving hold: a 1.5 to 2 percent push over 4 s `sine.inOut` at most. Cuts, not wipes.
- **Texture.** Paper grain multiply 8 to 12 percent, static, attached to the artwork; no film grain.

3D reading (Rasan3D, see references/3d.md section 5 "Paper / editorial"):
- A paper plane: `k.pxPlane(group, { x, y, w, h, texture, material: k.material("paper", { color: "#efe4d1" }) })` on a `k.layout({ at: 0, distance: 5 })` group (pxPlane takes composition pixels, y down); a second darker plane 0.2 m behind as wall. `k.rig("window", { dir: [-0.7, 0.4, 0.6], intensity: 1.0, shadows: true, shadowSoftness: 6 })`; `k.ground({ y, shadowOpacity })` as a shadow catcher.
- Camera keys: `lens: [[0, 85]]`, `fstop: 2.8`, `pos` arc 5 degrees over 4 s `power3.inOut`; a foreground paper edge out of focus; hold. Lift the page 12 mm over 600 ms `expo.out`; no spin.
- Optional line pass: `k.pass("sweep", { at: "post", frag, uniforms: { uProg: (t) => k.prog(t, 0.2, 0.9, "power2.inOut") } })` revealing a pre-drawn contour texture by arc-length, with `r3Grain`. Only if the 2D draw-on will not do.

## How to instruct a model to build it
```
Style: Kalighat pat, Calcutta bazaar painting, watercolour on paper. 1920x1080 HyperFrames, GSAP, one paused timeline.
Ground #efe4d1. One invented figure (a clerk with an umbrella) filling 80% of the height, slightly low, plain background.
Outline: ONE filled SVG path with a width profile (swells 14px to 6px, tapers at the ends), #1b201c.
Colour: flat rust #b54e1d, turmeric #d9ab53, sage #717a63, indigo #25407a; each with a darker edge wash and bare paper in the middle. No ground shadow.
Timeline: 0.0 contour draws (0.7s power2.inOut); 0.9 inner lines (0.4s power2.out); 1.1 flat colours fade (0.18s power1.out, stagger 0.06);
1.6 edge wash (0.36s power2.out); hold 1.2s; camera push 2% over the shot.
Negatives: no ornament-filled background, no uniform stroke, no gradient, no glow, no copying any living artist's eyes.
```
Claude draws a tapering line when asked for a filled path with a width profile; GPT models tend to return a uniform stroke, so give the profile numbers in the prompt (my observation from earlier library work, not a benchmark).

## Blending notes
- Carries: the one sweep, plain ground, wash volume, satire of contemporary figures.
- Pairs with **indian-matchbox-and-calendar-art** (its lithograph successors), **mughal-miniature-and-rajput-painting** (flat colour planes), **south-asian-shop-signage-and-ghat-lettering** (caption lettering), **paper-cut-and-papercraft** (paper as a physical plane).
- Breaks: ornament-filled grounds (becomes Madhubani), pictogram geometry (Warli), saturated chrome. Not library entries but also clashing: glass-ui.
