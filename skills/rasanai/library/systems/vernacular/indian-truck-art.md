## What it is
Truck art in South Asia began in the 1920s under British rule when trucks were first imported, with locals decorating them from regional folk traditions (Wikipedia). In India Punjab is described as a major centre, with a whole industry and multigenerational family businesses (Artzolo). The decoration is structural as well as painted: wood, chains, pendants, mirror work and calligraphy, and the artists are largely anonymous. A Vice piece follows Nafees Ahmad Khan, a truck artist in Indore, whose motifs include the tricolour, children's names, a mother's blessing, village women with water pots and the Taj Mahal. Wikipedia notes he designs one truck a day for over 32 years. For drivers the truck is a home away from home, decorated, in Artzolo's phrase, like a newlywed bride, and a portable shrine.

The tradition varies by region. Rooted Narratives describes Punjab as bold and joyous, Rajasthan as intricate patterns that echo miniature paintings and palace frescoes, the South as simpler, and Himalayan routes as cooler. Wikipedia singles out Uttar Pradesh for couplets and quartets ("truck literature") and Rajasthan and northern India for poetry and phool patti (flowers and leaves). Richard Frazer documents a distinct Kerala tradition of ultra-bold slab-serif lettering on carved name boards in bright colours, which is not found elsewhere in India.

## The rules that make it this and not something else
- **Paint order.** Base colour, then slogans, then icons and symbols (Artzolo). The order is part of the look and the best motion material.
- **Panels everywhere.** No empty surface. Rear, doors, bonnet and cab are cut into framed panels, each with one subject.
- **Safety slogans as a genre.** "Horn OK Please", "Blow Horn", "Use Dipper at Night" (Wikipedia, Artzolo), plus humorous or philosophical couplets. The slogan is spoken to the vehicle behind you.
- **Protective objects.** Nazar battu (evil-eye protection), lemon and green chillies on a string, black threads (Wikipedia, Artzolo), eagles, deities as mini shrines.
- **Enamel colour.** Hot yellow, orange, red as standard, dark colours avoided (Artzolo); enamel chosen for harsh climate (Rooted Narratives). Colour is said to carry meaning (saffron warmth, green prosperity, blue calm; from a blog not re-fetched, treat as unverified).
- **Many lettering styles per vehicle** (Artzolo: "written in various fonts"). Single-font typography is the opposite of the tradition.
- **Local specificity.** Rajasthan echoes miniature painting; Kerala has its own slab type; Pakistan's Karachi tradition (Wikipedia: "disco art" with flashing elements) is related but distinct. Do not collapse them into one look.

## Tokens decoded
- Measured from Commons photos of real trucks (k-means 6; weathered paint photographed in daylight, so saturation is lower than fresh enamel): base yellow `#daa824` / `#ddad21` (20% and 14% of two Indian truck photos), black `#080804`, rust-red `#a86a44` and olive `#6e6336` on a Rajasthan truck rear detail. Fresh-enamel values used in the palette (`#f2a900` base, `#d62b1f` red, `#1f9d55` green, `#1e5fb4` blue) are proposed: push the measured yellow toward full saturation and keep a hint of dust on top.
- Type: Rozha One (400, serif display, Latin and Devanagari) or Yatra One (400, display) for Devanagari slogans at 120 to 180 px in boxes with 6 px black outlines (`-webkit-text-stroke` or stacked `text-shadow`) and 8 px offset shadows; Teko (wght 300-700) for the plain-box instruction text; Alfa Slab One or Bevan (both 400) for a Kerala-style slab name board. Frazer describes vehicle lettering with drop shadows, reflections, shines, fat outlines and gradient fills, so one gradient fill inside the letter is period-correct on lettering (not on the ground). Use one slogan style per panel and a different style on the next. Hindi copy must be written by someone who reads Hindi.
- Grid: a 12 x 8 panel layout, not a type grid; panel gaps 10 px; the centre panel is double width.
- Shape: scallop-edged banners (CSS `mask` with repeating radial gradient, 36 px period), medallions, flower rows (SVG `<use>` of a single petal rotated at 30 degrees).
- Texture: gloss strip on enamel (a 4 px 30 percent white line along the top-left of each shape); chain and pendants as small SVG rows.
- Imagery: invented compositions with eagle, lotus, peacock and a roadside landscape; no brand logos.

## Motion and camera
2D:
- **Paint order animation (signature).** 0.0 s base colour wipes up from the bottom of the frame, 500 ms `power2.out`. 0.5 s slogan boxes stamp in at scale 1.18 to 1, 240 ms `back.out(1.4)`, 120 ms stagger. 1.3 s icons pop in (scale 0.8 to 1, 280 ms `back.out(1.2)`), stagger 90 ms. 2.0 s borders march round the frame: a dashed stroke, `stroke-dashoffset` from 100 to 0 over 900 ms `power2.inOut`. 3.0 s tassels swing.
- **Tassels (jhalar-like fringe).** `rotation` +-6 degrees at the pivot, 1.1 s `sine.inOut`, repeat 2 on a paused timeline (a finite repeat, no infinite loop), phase offset 80 ms per tassel. Wikipedia mentions hanging chains and mirror work; the term jhalar for fringe was not verified in any fetched source, so it is not used as a claim.
- **Road parallax.** Behind the truck back panel, road lines scroll at 0.6x the foreground; 3 depth layers; foreground dust 2 percent opacity at 0.2x.
- **Camera.** Locked tail-view at 35 mm equivalent with a 3 percent push over 3 s `sine.inOut` after the paint order completes; a rest of 600 ms.

3D reading (Rasan3D):
- `k.pxPlane` panels at z offsets 0, 0.04, 0.08 so a mirror disc or a medallion sits proud; panels in `k.material("matte", { color })` with a thin outline plane; mirror discs `k.material("chrome")` at radius 0.03 (needs the environment: `environment: { preset: "studio", intensity: 0.9 }`).
- Tassels as thin boxes hung from pivots (a `THREE.Group` per tassel) whose angle is a pure function of `t` via `k.at(t, [[0,0],[.55,6,"sine.inOut"],[1.1,-6,"sine.inOut"]])`, phase-shifted per tassel; if the pendants must swing with inertia use `k.simulate("pendants", { dt: 1/120, init, step })` (a damped pendulum) and read `sim.at(t)` in `pose`. A hanging chain is `k.tube(points, { radius: .004 })` in `k.material("chrome")`; a lemon-and-chilli string is a short `k.tube` with small `clay` spheres.
- Rig `window`, `dir: [-0.5, 0.6, 0.7]`, warm key; `k.ground` shadow catcher; the road is a 2D layer below.
- Lens 35 mm, a 6 degree arc over 3 s, f/4; one near foreground slab (a bumper plane) at z 1.0 gives parallax. No orbit.

## How to instruct a model to build it
```
Style: Indian truck rear panel, Punjab-style enamel on metal.
1920x1080 frame filled by panels: base #f2a900 (proposed), black 5px outlines, panels 10px gap.
Centre panel double width: eagle, lotus below. Top banner: scalloped, slogan in Rozha One 150px.
Slogans: "Horn OK Please" bottom, a short Hindi couplet supplied by a Hindi reader (do not invent).
Each text block uses a different lettering style; each has 6px black outline + 8px offset shadow.
Timeline (paused GSAP): 0.0 base wipes up (0.5s power2.out); 0.5 slogans stamp 1.18->1 (0.24s back.out(1.4), stagger 0.12);
1.3 icons pop (0.28s back.out(1.2), stagger 0.09); 2.0 border dash draws (0.9s power2.inOut);
3.0 tassels +-6deg (1.1s sine.inOut, finite repeat). 3.4-6.0 camera push 3% (sine.inOut).
Negatives: no gradient mesh, no glass, no thin type, no single-font layout, no fake Hindi.
```
Claude tends to harmonise everything to one font and a clean grid: tell it explicitly to vary lettering per panel and keep the black outline constant. GPT models often add glow and neon; tell them enamel is flat with a gloss line only.

## Blending notes
- Carries: panel structure, paint order, black outline, repeated borders, slogan lettering variety.
- Pairs with **south-asian-shop-signage-and-ghat-lettering** (same lettering culture), **tamil-cinema-posters** (enamel palette), **indian-matchbox-and-calendar-art** (deity and flower panels), **bollywood-hand-painted**.
- Breaks it: soft shadows, large empty space, a single typeface, desaturated or dark palettes.
Also clashes with (free text): glass UI, hairline type.
