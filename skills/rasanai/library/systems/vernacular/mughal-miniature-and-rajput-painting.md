## What it is
Mughal painting flourished from the 16th to the 19th century. Akbar's atelier (1556-1605) employed over a hundred painters between 1570 and 1585 and produced large illustrated manuscripts, including the Hamzanama (1562-1577), 1,400 cotton folios of about 27 x 20 inches that brought artists of different backgrounds into one working style. Jahangir's period brought finer brushwork and lighter colour and an interest in natural history and in European pictures; Shah Jahan's court painting became more rigid and formal. Named painters in the sources: Basawan, Ustad Mansur (botanical and animal studies), Govardhan (portrait), and Bichitr (European-influenced portraiture). The work is gouache (opaque watercolour) and gold on paper, with an elevated viewpoint and Persian-influenced decorative borders, and a stronger turn than Persian painting toward portrait and observed life.

Rajput painting grew from earlier Indian sources and favoured flat areas of bright colour, shallow space and decorative patterning. As Mughal patronage waned in the late 17th century, some court painters moved to the small Rajput kingdoms. Mewar kept "bright colour, flatness and abstract form" (Sahibdin, fl. 1628-55); Bundi and Kota took from Mughal work (Kota for hunting and elephants); Bikaner combined Mughal refinement with a Deccani-influenced palette; Kishangarh was cooler and more refined (Nihal Chand); the hill (Pahari) courts include the Nainsukh family of Guler, whose Gita Govinda series in the Tehri Garhwal style NYRB treats as a masterpiece. Themes: Ragamala (musical-emotional classifications), the Bhagavata Purana's Krishna stories, Ramayana episodes, and the sringara, which is love in its worldly and divine aspects, with works designed to kindle rasa. These are devotional and courtly works, and the Krishna and Ragamala paintings in particular are religious images.

## The rules that make it this and not something else
- **Flat planes, stacked.** Shallow space, a high horizon, planes of colour for ground, building and sky; figures set against the plane. Single-point perspective came from European influence, later, and sits against the "flattened multi-layered style" (Wikipedia).
- **The border is part of the picture.** Hashiya, the margin, was a specialised craft (search summaries); in the Shah Jahan Album borders under Jahangir carried flowers and animals, while under Shah Jahan they became more static and less naturalistic, emphasising pattern. The late album borders began to refer to the sitter's character. The album opened with a shamsa (sunburst) with mythical animals and gold foliage; calligraphic pages face painted ones.
- **Flat colour backgrounds in Rajput work.** Strong colours such as pure yellow or sage green with figures detached from them; stippled stars and silver moonlight for night (NYRB).
- **Time is elastic.** A figure may appear more than once in one frame in Rajput work (NYRB); Mughal work favours single moments of portrait or event.
- **Surface by burnishing.** Wasli paper layered and burnished with agate or cowrie shell until it is a smooth, polished surface; pigments applied in flat areas, thin glazes and stipples, with burnishing between stages; the finest details (eyelashes, border lines) with a squirrel-hair kalam (ArtFlute).
- **Palette.** Lapis blues, malachite and verdigris greens, cinnabar and red lead, shell gold, and Akbar-era peacock blue and Indian red (ArtFlute).
- **Mughal vs Rajput.** Mughal is naturalistic portraiture and observation; Rajput is mood, colour and gesture, with Pahari work adding lyric line (NYRB, Wikipedia).

## Tokens decoded
- Canvas: warm cream paper (#f1e4c3 proposed), with a margin band of 10 percent in a deep colour (lapis #1f3a8a proposed) carrying a thin gold rule and a repeating floral pattern (SVG pattern, 120 px repeat).
- Type: captions in a cartouche in Cormorant Garamond at 28 px or Amiri for Persian, never over faces. Hindi titles in Tiro Devanagari Hindi. Nasta'liq to be checked by a reader.
- Grid: picture field 84 percent of the frame, 8 percent margin; picture divided into 4 horizontal bands (sky 15, architecture 30, garden 35, ground 20). Elements snap to band edges.
- Shape: outlines 2 px #2a1d15; flat colour fills; no drop shadow; a 1 px pale highlight on gold areas.
- Texture: paper grain 256 px at 5 percent; a faint burnish sheen as a diagonal 4 percent white gradient on the picture field.
- Imagery: invented scenes (a garden pavilion at dusk, a musician with a bird); use the system, not specific folios, and not an artist's named composition.

## Motion and camera
2D:
- **Border first.** The border draws as a stroke around the frame in 900 ms (`sine.inOut`), then the pattern fades in at 5 percent per 200 ms.
- **Plane by plane (signature).** Ground, garden, building, figures, sky, each fades up 500 ms `sine.out` with a 200 ms stagger and a y-offset of 12 px to 0 (parallax-free: each plane only fades and drifts 12 px). Detail layer and gold last at 1800 ms, a 900 ms shimmer (a 40 px bright band masked to the gold lines, `power1.inOut`, once).
- **Small life.** One bird crosses 300 px in 2.4 s `sine.inOut` (an arc, not linear); a fountain's lines step in 100 ms steps; the figures are mostly still (a 6 px head turn over 700 ms, `sine.inOut`).
- **Folio pan.** A horizontal folio (2 or 3 scenes side by side) pans at 8 percent per second ease `sine.inOut` with a 700 ms rest on each scene; no zoom except to a face (T2, 1.0 to 1.8x, 1.2 s `power2.inOut`).
- Cuts between folios: a dissolve of 400 ms is allowed here (a page turn feel), otherwise cut.

3D reading (Rasan3D): layered flat planes with depth gaps and warm window light.
- Compose the picture as 5 to 8 `k.pxPlane(group, { x, y, w, h, texture })` layers (ground, garden, pavilion, figures, sky, border), spaced 0.12 m apart in z from a `k.layout({ at: 0, distance: 5 })` group, each with an alpha cut-out texture.
- `k.material("paper")` for the planes; one `k.rig("window", { dir: [-0.8, 0.45, 0.6], intensity: 1.1, shadows: true, shadowSoftness: 6 })` in a warm key so the planes cast soft shadows on the plane behind (the painted depth becomes a literal depth).
- Lens 85 mm, f/2.8 with focus on the figures plane; a 10 to 14 degree arc over 5 s (`sine.inOut`), parallax proves the depth; foreground: a curtain or a lattice plane at z 0.9 blurred. Border stays on the nearest plane as a frame. Hold 1 s at the end.
- No mesh garden, no glossy gold, no spin.
