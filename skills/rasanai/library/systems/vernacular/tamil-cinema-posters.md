## What it is
From around the 1950s, Tamil films in Chennai were advertised with hoardings, banners and tall cut-outs, hand-rendered by artists apprenticed to banner firms; posters were painted as artwork and then printed mechanically for distribution. The banner culture is described as born in Chennai and spread outward. K. Mohan (1926-2014) founded Mohan Arts in 1950, a firm for banners and cut-outs, and is recorded as making an 80-foot cut-out for Vanangamudi (1957); his sons Harinath Kumar and Narendran continue the firm. In the Coimbatore area, N. Velayutham Pillai painted banners through the 1950s to 1970s, and his son V. Jeevananthan (Jeeva, b. 1956) took over Cine Arts (est. 1954) after 1981 and has painted thousands of banners; he is also a painter and film writer whose book on cinema received a special mention at the 2011 National Film Awards.

This is a living working tradition with people who earn their living by it, not a historical oddity. In the 1980s and 1990s hand-painting was everywhere, with cinema hoardings over Triplicane and Royapettah; since the 2000s flex printing displaced much of it, and one painter in DT Next notes that hand-painted work lasts 10 to 15 years where flex lasts about six months.

## The rules that make it this and not something else
- **Scale first.** Cut-outs are reported to reach 60 feet; a Rajinikanth banner in Coimbatore was 10 x 10 feet. The hero is not a picture on the street, it is a building-scale presence. Composition obeys that: the figure is the largest element by a clear factor.
- **Made by a workshop.** Studios employed 10 to 15 workers including tailors and carpenters, with about three days to finish a banner (The News Minute). Materials named: gada (heavy) cloth, wooden frames, water coat, vajram (gum), chalk, linseed oil. In DT Next: bamboo tati boards, distemper primer, hand-mixed pigments and enamels.
- **Projection and grid.** The News Minute records a magic-lantern projection method in use in the 1970s and 1980s to transfer images; earlier work was gridded. Treat the enlarged, simplified face as a consequence of the method.
- **Colour.** Early work in primary colours (red, black, blue); later fluorescent colours (DT Next). Colour is assigned to emotional role: hero in warm skin and a hot costume against a cool field.
- **Price and speed.** Rs 10 to 50 per square foot in the 1980s and 1990s (DT Next): speed forced clear shapes and few colours.
- **Poster as a design object.** Posters are a separate, designed track. The News Minute notes S.S. Vasan's 1942 poster competitions and treats Chandralekha as the breakthrough film for banner culture; it also points to Mani Ratnam's Bombay and Nayagan as cited poster examples.
- **Script.** Titles are in Tamil script, hand-lettered with outlines and shadow. Tamil's vertical vowel signs and curved forms mean letter spacing rules differ from Latin; letterspacing breaks readability.

## Tokens decoded
- Canvas: a flat saturated ground with a darker band behind the title. Proposed hexes in the frontmatter (not sourced).
- Type: Tiro Tamil at 200 to 260 px as a base for a title where the family reads well; for an outlined hand-lettered feel, stack a 10 px outline in #0e0e12 and an offset shadow 16 px in #e12b1f. For heavier weights use Baloo Thambi 2 at 700 or 800 (as I know the family). Never track Tamil; `letter-spacing: 0` and `font-kerning: normal`. Keep `lang="ta"` and `font-feature-settings: normal`.
- Latin subtitles: set the English line in a separate Latin face such as Anton (Google Fonts) rather than relying on Tamil-face Latin glyphs.
- Grid: hero at one third, cut at the knee, low camera; supporting group in a diagonal behind; 12 percent safe margin for the title band.
- Shape: hard outline; a drop shadow at 45 degrees; flat enamel highlights as 2 or 3 white strokes on the hero's costume.
- Imagery: invented heroes, not specific living actors; give the hero a pose (raised arm, a turn, a stride), a prop, and a symbolic ground (a sunrise disc, a city skyline).

## Motion and camera
2D:
- **The raise.** The hero plate enters from the bottom edge: y +110 percent to 0 over 900 ms `expo.out`, with a 1.04 overshoot-free settle (no `back`), as if hoisted. Title follows at +200 ms: scale 1.15 to 1 over 320 ms `expo.out`.
- **Flank.** Supporting plates slide in from the sides, x +-80 px to 0 over 500 ms `power3.out`, stagger 100 ms.
- **Border flash.** A 6 px painted border rectangle draws on over 400 ms `power2.out` stroke-dashoffset; holds. No glow.
- **Camera.** Low-angle: apply `rotateX(-6deg)` on the world with `perspective: 1400px`, hold, then a 5 percent push over 2.2 s `power2.inOut` on the hero (depth 1.4) with the ground at 0.5.
- **Cut-out row.** Lateral truck along a row of hero cut-outs in stages: each leg 900 ms `power1.inOut`, with a 400 ms rest at each figure.

3D reading (Rasan3D): the cut-out as a standing plywood plane in a street.
- `k.pxPlane(group, { x, y, w, h, texture })` for the painted figure, plus an edge: an extruded silhouette from `k.svg` with `depth` 0.04 and `k.material("matte")` for the edge, a `k.ground` shadow catcher for a hard shadow.
- Rig `low-key` with `{ dir: [-0.5, 0.35, 0.8], key: "#fff1d6", rim: "#ff8a3d", intensity: 1.1 }` (key/fill/rim are colours, intensity the one number); lens 24 mm low at y 0.4 and a crane up 0.8 m over 3 s (`power3.inOut`); f/4. Foreground: a bunting plane at z 1.5 slightly out of focus.
- Never an orbit around the cut-out; a single push and a rest.
