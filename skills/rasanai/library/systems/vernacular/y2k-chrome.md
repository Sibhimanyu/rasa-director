## What it is
Y2K chrome is the look of consumer technology at the turn of the millennium: translucent coloured plastic, chrome, bubbles and capsule buttons. Wikipedia's Y2K aesthetic entry names the traits: lime, orange and hot pink with white and metallic chrome, clear or translucent casings sometimes with glitter, gradients, chunky or rounded fonts, 3D elements and metallic or glossy effects, with the iMac G3 as the exemplar "blobject". It followed Memphis and preceded Frutiger Aero. The mood is optimistic and a little ironic: technology as a candy you can see into.

## The rules that make it this and not something else
- The material is the message: translucent plastic with visible internals, or chrome with real reflections. Gloss without translucency or reflection is generic "shiny".
- Candy colour, one per frame. The iMac G3 shipped in blueberry, strawberry, tangerine, grape and lime in January 1999 (the first, Bondi Blue, came from greenish-blue beach glass); keep a single saturated hue against white and chrome.
- Rounded everything. Capsule buttons, blob silhouettes, soft shadows under objects. No sharp corners except on chrome type.
- UI wears its gloss: Aqua described as water-themed, droplet-like components, reflective and translucent, "lickable" (David Pogue's phrase is quoted on Wikipedia; Jobs's was "it's liquid").
- Gradients are allowed and expected, but they follow a form (a highlight on top, a darker core, a reflected rim below).
- The object is the hero; the type is a caption on a plate.

## Tokens decoded
- Canvas: pale cool white `#eef3f8` (proposed, not sourced) or white with a faint blue vignette. Ink: deep navy `#0b1a33`. Candy accent: tangerine `#e34905` and Bondi Blue `#528ba2` are measured from iMac G3 photographs; hot pink `#ff3d8b`, lime `#7dff3a`, blueberry `#2b6cff` are proposed screen hues. A measured photograph reads darker and less saturated than the lit CSS version: the plastic is translucent, so for screen, lift the saturation 10-15% and keep the white core.
- Chrome ramp (for 2D faux-chrome only; real chrome uses the 3D environment): `linear-gradient(180deg,#ffffff 0%,#c9d1dc 38%,#6d7683 50%,#e8edf3 62%,#aab3c0 100%)` with a hard horizon line at 50%, the tell of real reflective metal.
- Type: Michroma or Audiowide for technical headlines (single weight 400, OFL); Fredoka (variable `wght`) for bubble words; body in Nunito Sans. Original Aqua UI face was Lucida Grande (sourced; proprietary), so use Nunito Sans or Open Sans as the free stand-in.
- Radii: pill 999px for buttons, 28px for plates. Inner highlight `inset 0 1px 0 rgba(255,255,255,.9)`, outer rim `0 0 0 1px rgba(11,26,51,.18)`.
- Gloss plate recipe: top half lighter than bottom half (a hard split at 50%), with a 60% white highlight ellipse near the top.

## Motion and camera
2D: elements arrive like drops of liquid. Pill buttons scale from 0.6 to 1 with `back.out(1.7)` over 450 ms, settle with a 120 ms squash (`scaleY 0.94 -> 1`, `power2.out`). A highlight band (a skewed white gradient, 18% wide) travels across a plate in 700 ms with `power2.inOut`, once per hold, never looping. Window or card exits use a genie-style squeeze: scale X to 0.1 and Y to 0.02 while moving to a dock point over 500 ms (`power3.in`); Aqua's genie effect is the sourced reference. Ripples: three concentric rings, 600 ms, `sine.out`, opacity 0.5 to 0. Held beats 700-1200 ms so the surface can be read.

3D (Rasan3D reading): this look wants real space.
- Hero as `k.material('chrome', {color:'#d7dde6'})` on a rounded blob (RoundedBoxGeometry with large radius) or an extruded wordmark (`k.extrudeText`, bevel high, depth about 8% of cap height), or `k.material('gummy', {color:'#ff3d8b'})` for a candy translucent shell. Put a smaller solid object inside the gummy shell to get the iMac "see the innards" read.
- Rig `rim` with `environment: {preset:'studio', intensity: 1.0}` for chrome (metal is its reflections; give it bright panels to reflect); `three-point` with a warm rim for gummy, with an opaque `background` set so transmission has something to refract.
- Lens 85 mm, `fstop` 2.8, camera 3-5% push over 2.4 s with `power3.inOut`, then hold. A 24 mm only for a fly-through between scenes.
- Ground: `k.ground({shadowOpacity:.3})` over a pale 2D floor; the shadow settles a frame after the object lands.
- Post: `bloom {strength:.35, threshold:1.15}` on a single emissive accent only, `grain .02`. Never bloom the chrome itself.
- Not this: a lone chrome sphere spinning in a void. Put the object on a surface, tilt the light 30 degrees, and let a second object or the UI plate sit in the same light.

## Blending notes
- Carries into a blend: the translucent or chrome material, pill radii, one candy hue, the genie and highlight-sweep motion.
- With frutiger-aero: shares gloss and optimism; keep Y2K's candy colour and drop Frutiger's nature photography, or you get both at once and neither.
- With pixel-game-ui or vaporwave-and-web-nostalgia: use Y2K for the hero object and the other for UI chrome and type.
- Breaks: Swiss-style rigid grids, hairline type, matte ceramic palettes, any muted restraint.

## Cheap tells (additional)
- A purple-to-pink flat gradient with no translucency: the original is a material you can see into.
- Chrome as a linear grey gradient with no horizon split or environment.
