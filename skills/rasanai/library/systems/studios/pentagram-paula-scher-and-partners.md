## What it is
Pentagram is a design partnership founded on 12 June 1972 in Notting Hill, London, by Alan Fletcher, Theo Crosby, Colin Forbes, Kenneth Grange and Mervyn Kurlansky. It is run as a flat partnership of designers who own the firm equally, and it has partners in London, New York, San Francisco, Berlin and Austin (per Wikipedia at fetch time). The partner model matters for a library: there is no single Pentagram look, only partners' voices. This entry takes Paula Scher (partner since 1991) as the loud, type-first voice and uses Michael Bierut (partner since 1990, ten years at Vignelli's office before) as the quiet, systematic counterpoint.

Scher's Public Theater identity (begun 1994; Pentagram dates the work 1996 on her profile page and 1994 to 2004 for the wood-type phase on the project page) is the reference. Pentagram describes it as a graphic language that reflects street typography in an extremely active, unconventional, almost graffiti-like juxtaposition. Wood type inspired by Victorian theatre posters was set against hard colour fields.

## The rules that make it this and not something else
- Type is the picture. Letters are scaled until the frame crops them; a word can be a wall.
- Two or three colours per piece, flat. Wikipedia summarises her palettes as bold, limited, often two or three colours.
- A single condensed or wood-type voice for display. Pentagram's Public page records the evolution: wood faces (1994 to 2004), Akzidenz-Grotesk (2005, the 50th anniversary), then Hoefler & Frere-Jones Knockout with redrawn letterforms (2008).
- 2008 refinement: De Stijl inspired grids and angled printer's rules, the lockup moved from vertical to horizontal, "more architectural", but strict 90 degree angles and Victorian wood block elements were kept.
- Applied at every scale: posters, scaffolding signage, environmental graphics, advertising across New York (as listed on the Public project page).
- Maximalism as a stated position: Scher on maps, "Less is more and more is more. It's the middle that's not a good place" (quoted on Wikipedia).
- Bierut's counter-mode (from his profile): systematic identities for Mastercard, Slack, Verizon, Disney; the 2016 Clinton "H"; a typographic installation for the Obama Presidential Center tower. Quiet, rule-based, one idea executed everywhere.

## Motion and camera
Pentagram publishes posters, not motion. Everything below is proposed, translated from the poster logic.

2D (HyperFrames, craft.md vocabulary): treat the frame as a poster that is re-set.
- A colour block wipes in from a frame edge (`clip-path: inset()` or `scaleX` from the edge, 0.32 s `power3.out`); type lands in two or three pieces on separate offsets (0.06 s stagger, `yPercent` 100 to 0 inside masks, `expo.out`), then holds 0.7 to 1.2 s before the replacement.
- Scale push: a word scales 0.85 to 1.12 over 0.9 s `power2.inOut` until the frame crops it (transform-origin on the word's left edge so it bleeds off the right). No bounce, no blur. Cuts on the beat are often better than tweens.
- Rules: 6 to 14 px bars at 0 and 90 degrees, drawn with `scaleX`/`scaleY` 0.25 s; one deliberate rule at 12 to 20 degrees per scene.
- Camera: locked; the only camera move is the type's own push-in. Grade: none, flat print colour with a 2 to 3 percent paper-grain overlay if the ground is paper. Sound: a block-thud on each wipe, music cuts on the beat.
3D (Rasan3D, 3d.md sections 3, 5, 18): wood-type letters as signage standing in a street.
- `k.extrudeText("PUBLIC", { font: <TTF path>, size: 2.2, depth: 0.35, bevel: 0.01, material: k.material("paper", { color: "#ee3c15" }) })` (or `matte`), letters in a row on a `k.ground({ color: "#e7e3ee" })` plane, `k.rig("window", { dir: [-0.7, 0.6, 0.4], shadowSoftness: 2 })` (hard-ish shadow from one key), `environment: "soft"`, no bloom.
- Camera keys: `lens: [[0, 70]]`, `pos: [[0, [-6, 1.2, 8]], [4, [6, 1.2, 8], "power1.inOut"]]`, `target: [[0, [-3, 1, 0]], [4, [3, 1, 0], "power1.inOut"]]`: a lateral track past the word like a sign being walked past; `fstop: 8` so all letters stay sharp. Letters cropped by the frame edge on purpose.
- Never glossy: this look is printed and painted. Pin one small grotesk caption with `k.pinDom` in the 2D UI layer.

## How to instruct a model to build it
"Build a typographic poster-in-motion in the manner of Paula Scher's Public Theater work. Canvas flat #e7e3ee; ink #111111; two accents only, #ee3c15 and #7380c6. Display in Oswald 700 uppercase (stand-in for Knockout), line-height 0.88, set so one word fills 80 percent of frame width and is cropped by the right edge. A 10 px black rule at 90 degrees under the word, one rule at 14 degrees crossing a colour block. Body in Schibsted Grotesk 600, 4.5 percent of short side, flush left. Motion: colour block wipes in from the left 0.32 s power3.out; word lands in two parts 0.06 s apart; hold 0.9 s; scale 1.0 to 1.12 over 0.9 s power2.inOut; hard cut to the next word on the beat." Claude tends to centre and add margin: say "flush left, bleed off the edge, no safe margin on display type". GPT-style models often add a gradient or soft shadow: ban both. (Carried from the earlier pass.)

## Blending notes
Carries: edge-to-edge scale, two-colour discipline, the printer's rule as a structural device, wood-type caps. Pairs with `swiss-international` structure (take the grid, keep the volume) and with `dia-studio-kinetic-type` motion (take the crop and scale, add continuous transition), `push-pin-studios`, `collins-studio`. Breaks with luxury minimalism and with anything soft. Bierut's mode is the safer blend when the film must feel institutional: one colour, one typeface, one idea, repeated.
