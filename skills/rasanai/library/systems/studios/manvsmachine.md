## What it is
ManvsMachine (mvsm.com: "Design & Direction Co.", "a multi-dimensional creative company specialised in the fields of design, film and visual arts") was founded in 2007 in Shoreditch by Mike Alderson and Tim Swift (It's Nice That, Incase). Landor, a WPP agency, took a majority stake on 2 November 2015; a Los Angeles studio followed in 2016. Staff is about 60 across both cities, about 90 percent full-time, and roughly 70 percent of work comes direct from clients (Communication Arts). Early broadcast work: ITV2 rebrand and Film4 idents (late 2014). Recent: Ford Mustang GTD with Wieden+Kennedy (audio Echolab), Robinhood, Magnum, Moncler, Squarespace, Cash App, Nike Air Max, Renault R5, Lexus. Its name is its method: concept and technique are "equally important. Always has been" (Alderson, Communication Arts).

## The rules that make it this and not something else
- "Design by doing": stop motion, hand-drawn and cel animation sit beside CG; ideas are made, not only drawn (Communication Arts).
- Collision of the conceptual and the technical; staff are "a thinker-who-makes or a maker-who-thinks"; "under promise, over deliver" (Incase).
- CG is a lens-based discipline here: Mustang GTD was entirely CG yet shot as "handheld camera techniques, unconventional angles, and a high-octane edit", "an aesthetic that celebrated imperfections" (STASH). The camera is characterised, not neutral.
- One hue world per film (measured, see palette evidence): deep black floor, midtones in the hero hue, a 1 to 2 percent accent (Mustang red #a31305), or a saturated whole-frame field (R5 amber at 64 percent).
- Their own site is restrained: #231f20 rules and links, a #0e1adf loading overlay with a white logo; the work carries the colour, the chrome is quiet.
- Position on AI (Communication Arts, Andrew Hopper): "just another tool", keep creative decisions human; replacing lens-based photography with it "starts to feel wrong".
- Type is not documented in the sources; treat all typographic tokens as proposed.

## Tokens decoded
- Floor: #010102 to #05070c (measured blacks, 25 to 33 percent of the frame). Mid-hero hue: #2a384a / #424b59 (Mustang) or #0b1d2b / #1e4455 (Robinhood). Light: #a9b4b4 (Robinhood highlights). Accent: #a31305, at most 2 percent of pixels.
- Display type (proposed): Inter Tight 600, tracking -0.02em, 3 to 5 percent of short side, sentence case. Not a hero element.
- Lighting: one key, one rim, a bounce card; dark floor; roughness 0.2 to 0.6; no default chrome.
- Camera: lens 24 to 35 mm with handheld for power products; 50 to 100 mm slow for beauty and macro.
- Durations: 120 to 300 ms for hits and cuts on an aggressive piece, 1.2 to 2.4 s moves on a sculptural one (proposed; no frame count was measured).

## Motion and camera
2D (HyperFrames): hero is a pre-rendered or photographed plate; GSAP does captions and a grade. Handheld micro-shake on the world: seeded noise, 1 to 3 px and 0.1 to 0.4 degrees at 1 to 2 Hz (vocabulary: Handheld). Speed ramp inside a child timeline: progress tween `expo.inOut`, fast, slow on the hit, fast. Match cuts on shape or motion. Caption resolves with a mask-rise 400 ms `power3.out`; hold at least 700 ms; exit 250 ms `power2.in`. Colour field wipe in the hero hue on a downbeat. Grade: crush blacks to #010102, lift one hue world, add grain 0.02.

## How to instruct a model to build it
"ManvsMachine-style brand film. First write the concept in one sentence, then pick ONE hue world from the product (for example blue-black with a red accent, or amber). Grade every frame into it: blacks at #010102, midtones in the hue, accent at 2 percent of pixels. CG hero, ceramic or brushed-metal material (no chrome unless it reflects something designed), `low-key` rig with one key, a rim and a bounce, `fstop` 2.0. Decide the camera's character: handheld imperfection (roll 0.3 degrees, 1 to 2 Hz) for power products, slow `sine.inOut` truck for beauty. One rack focus per shot, no orbit, no turntable. Cut on action with a speed ramp. Type is Inter Tight 600, small, a caption to the object, 400 ms power3.out resolve. If the concept implies a real shoot, treat plates as photographed, not generated." Claude plans lights and lenses well when asked for named lights; GPT-style output tends to fall back to turntable spins and rainbow gradients, so ban both.
