## What it is
Buck is an American design-led animation and production company founded in 2004 by Jeff Ellermeyer, Ryan Honey and Orion Tait; its name nods to Buckminster Fuller (Wikipedia; Communication Arts says the Los Angeles HQ, a former factory and movie backlot, "seems imbued with the utopian design sensibility" of its namesake). It is best understood as a method rather than a look: a style-agnostic studio that chooses the medium that best carries the idea, with 2D, 3D, live action, AR and illustration systems in one house (Communication Arts: Instagram AR filters, Facebook's Alegria illustration and animation system, Tinder's 75 second "Invention of Together" with 3D characters). Offices: Los Angeles, Brooklyn, Sydney (2015), Amsterdam (2021), London.

## The rules that make it this and not something else
- **Medium follows idea.** The idea is one clear transformation or conceit, then the medium that sells it. There is no house style to apply, which is why a Buck-style piece has to start from the idea, not the palette.
- **Previs first, craft second.** An interview summary (Motion Array, not fetched) says "Come Together" was animated in 3D first for timing, shapes built in vector, laser-cut from wood and paper, painted and shot in stop motion against the previs. Treat as secondary. The transferable rule: lock timing in a blockout, then build layers to it.
- **A flexible identity that is "both well-behaved and irreverent"** (Buck's own rebrand text). The rebrand wordmark is built from shapes sharing one architecture that "come together to make a whole", after Fuller; Mabry was chosen as the primary typeface and its glyphs expanded into "an expressive palette of elements to choose from" (buck.co/work/buck-rebrand).
- **Experimental ethos and a willingness to push a client.** In Communication Arts a Tinder creative lead credits Buck's "thoughtfulness, the pushes, the experimentation": the weird idea is presented, not pre-filtered.
- **In-house depth.** Buck grows talent in-house; a shared shorthand makes consistent timing across layers possible. For a film: one timing sheet that every layer obeys.
- Sound is part of the identity; Antfood appears as a collaborator in Buck's work list (AMP Awards titles).

## Tokens decoded
- Brand type: Mabry (commercial). In a film use Schibsted Grotesk 500/700 or Familjen Grotesk, tracking -0.02em for display; build the expressive letter-shapes (quarter circles, half discs, bars) as SVG so they can morph.
- Palette: per project, but flat. Working range from Buck's own project colours: red #f32c17, blue #196efa, yellow #fceb2d, violet #9083b5, pink #ffb7b4, ochre #f4c054, green #508054; paper white or black ground. Large flat fields, two or three per piece.
- Shapes: circles, quarter-circles, triangles and rectangles on an 8 px unit (proposed); letters built from the same set.
- Shadows only where real physical depth exists: cut-paper layers with a hard 2 to 6 px offset at 12 to 20 percent black (proposed); otherwise none.

## Motion and camera
Timings are proposed; no Buck timing sheet is public. 2D (HyperFrames/GSAP; vocabulary.md section 6 and 7): the transformation is the idea. Shape A morphs to object B over 400 to 800 ms `power3.inOut` (SVG path morph or clip-path), hold 300 to 600 ms; secondary elements offset 40 to 80 ms with `stagger: { each: 0.05, from: "center" }`; follow-through overshoot 4 to 8 percent with `back.out(1.2)` only on landings. A `#world` wrapper takes a 4 to 8 percent push, `power2.inOut`, 1.2 to 2 s. For a stop-motion feel step the timeline on twos with `steps()` or by rounding time to 1/12 s in a tween `onUpdate` (see paper-cutout-parallax-2-5d, stop-motion-laika-replacement).
3D (Rasan3D, references/3d.md): the previs step is itself a Rasan3D scene: grey `k.material("matte")` primitives, `k.rig("three-point")`, camera keys `pos`/`target`/`lens` at 35 to 50 mm with the final eases, so the move is locked before styling. Then restyle: flat colour fields as `k.material("unlit", { color })` with `toneMapping: "none"` for graphic flatness, or `k.material("paper", { color })` with `k.rig("window", { shadows: true, shadowSoftness: 2 })`. Craft look: 3 to 5 planes at z spacing 0.04 to 0.1, each with `castShadow`, camera dolly 0.3 m over 4 s `sine.inOut` so parallax comes from real depth, `fstop: 5.6` to blur the back plane. A transformation can run in a raymarched pass (`k.pass` with `r3SMin` blending a sphere into a box from `uMorph(t)`), which gives a true metaball-style morph between two forms.

## How to instruct a model to build it
Paste-ready brief: "Buck-style design-led animation. State the single transformation that carries the idea (for example: a ring of shapes becomes the product). Block the whole piece in plain boxes first with timing in ms, then build final layers to that timing. Flat saturated colours as large areas on white or black, two or three accents from #f32c17, #196efa, #fceb2d, no gradients. Typography is a rigorous grotesque (Schibsted Grotesk 500) with tracking -0.02em; letters may be built from the same geometric shapes as the imagery. Motion: morphs 400 to 800 ms power3.inOut, holds 300 to 600 ms, secondary offsets 40 to 80 ms. If the look is physical craft, stack 3 to 5 offset cut-paper layers with hard 2 to 6 px shadows and animate on twos." Claude tends to use one ease on everything; require different offsets and a hold after every beat.

## Blending notes
Carries: previs discipline, transformation as idea, craft layers, colour as large flat fields. Mixes with Swiss grids and ManvsMachine polish. Because Buck is a method, use it as a process layer on another look, not as a palette. Cheap-imitation risk is highest when the idea is missing: a morph with nothing to say.
