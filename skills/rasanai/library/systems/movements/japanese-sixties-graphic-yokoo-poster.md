## What it is
In the 1960s Japanese graphic design was led by Bauhaus- and Swiss-influenced abstraction (Kamekura, Tanaka, the Nippon Design Center, founded 1960). Tadanori Yokoo (b. 1936, Nishiwaki; to Tokyo around 1960; stage designer for avant-garde theatre; Push Pin Studio and Kurosawa as influences, per Wikipedia) broke from it in 1965 with a hanged self-portrait poster, "Made in Japan / Having reached a climax at the age of 29, I was dead", then with silkscreen posters for Hijikata's butoh, Terayama's Tenjo Sajiki, Kara Juro's Situation Theatre and Sogetsu Art Center events. Friedman Benda describes the work as "bold use of collage, psychedelia and appropriation of popular cultural icons, both Japanese and Western". MoMA included him in "Word and Image" (1968) and gave him a solo show in 1972; MoMA's post essay calls the posters "psychedelic and darkly fantastic" and places them at the border of art, design and politics. thegraphicdesignschool.com records that he rejected Swiss grid restraint as foreign to Japanese pictorial tradition.

## The rules that make it this and not something else
- Collage as method: ukiyo-e waves and sun rays, advertising, postcards, film stills as halftone photographs, the author's own face. Nothing is one image; everything is a found piece.
- Flat inks, butted and overprinted. In the measured Koshimaki-Osen poster: pink #d4618a (17%), cyan-blue #2181ab (12%), red #c03434 (9%), black #05060a (8%), gold #cf9140 (6%), the remainder halftone greys and paper. Saturation is the point; value contrast comes from black outlines and the grey photographs.
- Composition by radiating stripes. Rising-sun rays behind a focal group; a bold title band above (Koshimaki-Osen: roughly the top 18%, extruded Latin title in gold with a blue and black stepped shadow, a kanji plate beneath it); text columns in boxes at the sides.
- Mixed scripts and vertical text in one frame.
- Black-and-white halftone photographs against saturated drawn parts: the photographic grey is the rest area.
- Irony and menace under pop colour: death, theatre, politics, eroticism.
- Cooper Hewitt names the "signature fluorescent color scheme" and the recurring rising-sun motif; no source quantifies the inks, so values above are measured from a JPEG reproduction only.

## Tokens decoded
- Inks as roles: A pink #d4618a (push to #ff2d8a for screen, proposed), B cyan-blue #2181ab, C red #c03434, D gold #cf9140 (push to #f7b500, proposed), black #05060a, paper #d8d6d8. Each frame uses all four inks, none dominating beyond 25%.
- Title: "Archivo Black" or "Rubik Mono One" 400, upper case, 140-200px; extrusion = 6-8 stacked text-shadow copies stepping 3px down-right in blue #2181ab then a final black one. Japanese plate: "Dela Gothic One" 400 on a pale blue plate with a 4px black border. Small text: "Zen Kaku Gothic New" 500 at 16-18px in bordered boxes.
- Halftone: photographs converted to dots at 45 degrees, 6-10 px cell at 1920x1080 (proposed; the measured reproduction is too small to count dots).
- Frame: 14-20px red or black border around everything, content inside.

## Motion and camera
2D (HyperFrames HTML/CSS/GSAP, vocabulary.md texture and transition terms):
- Panels stamp in: scale 0.82 to 1 plus 4 to 0 degree rotation, 0.24 s `back.out(1.7)`, stagger 0.08-0.12 s, in z-order from back to front.
- Ray burst: a 24-ray `conic-gradient` or SVG wedge group behind the focal figures, `rotation` 0 to 20deg over 4 s on `none` (linear), or scale 0.6 to 1.1 over 0.5 s `power3.out` on the hit. Alternating ray colours swap by CSS variable.
- Colour-swap beat: swap the four ink roles (A, B, C, D rotate one place) every 0.48 or 0.96 s with `tl.set` on CSS variables; hard, no crossfade; 2-frame white flash optional.
- Photo layers hold on twos (an update every 83 ms at 30 fps) with 1-2 px jitter; halftone via an SVG pattern mask or a canvas dot layer.
- Camera: locked; occasional 6-10% punch-in 0.4 s `power3.out`; hard cuts between full colourways, 2-frame colour-block cut.
3D (Rasan3D, references/3d.md sections 5, 12, 15):
- Cards: `k.pxPlane(group, { x, y, w, h, texture: k.image(url) })` or `k.panel({ texture, depth: 0.01 })` for each printed layer, `k.material("unlit")` for ink fields; 6-8 cards 0.1-0.3 m apart; ray burst as one large `k.svg` in `unlit`.
- Camera `lens: [[0, 85]]` (flattened perspective), pos a push `[[0,[0,0,9]],[3,[0.5,0,7],"power3.inOut"]]`; rotate the stack with `roll` or the group 8-15 degrees on Y over 1.2 s `power2.inOut`; no `fstop`.
- Look: `post: { grain: 0.02 }`, no bloom; a post pass `k.pass("halftone", { at: "post", frag })` using `r3HalftoneFW(gl_FragCoord.xy, r3Luma(c), 7.0, 0.7854)` on the luminance of photographic areas only (mask by a render target or an alpha channel), and `r3Dither(c, gl_FragCoord.xy, 6.0)` to flatten tones to ink.
- Type and the panel plates stay DOM (`k.pinDom`).

## How to instruct a model to build it
"Make a Tokyo 1966 silkscreen theatre-poster collage. Black ground, thin red frame. Top band: title in Archivo Black upper case with a blue then black stepped shadow, beneath it a pale blue plate with Japanese in Dela Gothic One. Middle: 24-ray rising-sun burst (red and white) behind a black-and-white halftone photograph of two figures, 8 px dot, 45 degrees. Flat inks only: #d4618a, #2181ab, #c03434, #cf9140 (brighter if the screen can take it), each 10-20% of the frame, no gradients, no glow. Pink drawn motif (a peach, a floating figure) at the bottom with a wave in blue and a train. Text in bordered boxes at both sides, vertical and horizontal. Motion: panels stamp in 0.24 s back.out(1.7) staggered 0.1 s, the burst turns linearly, the four inks rotate roles every 0.48 s on the beat, hard cuts." Models tend to tidy this into a symmetrical grid and to add neon glow; list "overlap, crowding, no glow" as negatives. Image-first models fake the halftone with blur: ask for real dots.

## Blending notes
Carries: ray burst behind a halftone photograph, flat butted inks, extruded title, ink-role swaps. With ukiyo-e use a Hokusai wave as one found object in the collage, keep Yokoo's colour logic. With psychedelic-poster it merges easily (shared saturated vibrating colour) but Western psychedelic lettering is illegible by design, Yokoo's is legible. With Swiss it clashes; use one calm Swiss data frame as an intentional counter-beat, not an average. Breaks with restrained tracking, white space, soft UI.
