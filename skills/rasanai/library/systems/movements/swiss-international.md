## What it is
A design approach that grew out of Switzerland from the 1930s-50s and peaked in the 1950s-60s, carried abroad by the journal New Graphic Design (founded 1958-59; Müller-Brockmann co-founded it with Neuburg, Lohse and Vivarelli) and by American adoption, notably at MIT. It treats the grid as "the most legible and harmonious means for structuring information". Sans-serif type set flush left, asymmetric layouts and objective photography replace ornament and illustration. In motion terms it is the system whose only drama is scale, position and timing on a visible structure.

## The rules that make it this and not something else
- A grid is constructed before anything is placed and every element sits on it. Müller-Brockmann's Grid Systems (1981) shows grids from 8 to 32 fields, and its own cover is composed on a 32-field grid (sourced from the Peoples Graphic Design Archive record).
- Asymmetry with a strong left axis. Centred composition is the fastest way out of the style.
- One grotesque family. Akzidenz-Grotesk was the early choice; Helvetica (1957) and Univers followed. The Der Film poster (1960) is set in Akzidenz-Grotesk with a small line in Monotype Grotesque Bold 216 (Fonts In Use).
- Hierarchy by scale and weight only: large size jumps, no effects.
- Photography chosen for objectivity, or no image at all. Der Film uses no picture: the word film is overprinted with the article der at different exposures and sharpness to imply cinematic montage (Fonts In Use, quoting Barbara Junod). Tonhalle posters from 1950 grew "increasingly abstract and focused on the feelings of the music", expressing rhythm through scale and repetition.
- Colour is restricted. Measured: the Beethoven poster (22 Feb 1955) is black 46% and white 42% only; the Musica Viva poster (1959) is a pale paper ground with grey; the Grid Systems cover is a single flat orange. One hue at a time.
- Type is part of the image: the picture is made of letters, circles and rules, not placed beside them.

## Tokens decoded
- canvas #e8e9dd (measured paper), ink #000000, white #fefefe, grey #b9bbbc, accent #fc5b02 (measured orange). Use the accent once per scene as a full flat field or a single word, never as a gradient. Red or blue accents are legitimate in the period but their hexes here are proposals.
- Display: Hanken Grotesk 700 or Schibsted Grotesk 700 for Akzidenz colour; Inter Tight 700 for Helvetica colour; tracking -0.02em large, 0 small. Body Inter 400.
- Grid (proposed adaptation for 1920x1080): 8 columns x 4 rows = 32 fields, outer margin left 120px, right 60px, top and bottom 60px, gutter 24px, baseline 8px. Type sizes from a ratio scale, e.g. 16 / 32 / 64 / 160px (proposed).
- Rules: 2px ink, full width or column width, as dividers and as the draw-on accent.

## Motion and camera
All timings are proposals; the sources are static print.
2D (HyperFrames HTML/CSS/GSAP): put the grid in the DOM as CSS grid with `gap: 24px` and place every element by `grid-column` / `grid-row`; an optional overlay of 1px lines at 15% opacity reveals the grid during an opening 600ms (lines draw `scaleX 0 to 1`, `power2.inOut`, 40ms stagger) then fades. Text enters by line mask reveal: each line in an `overflow:hidden` wrapper, `yPercent 100 to 0`, 450ms `power3.out`, 70ms stagger in reading order. Rules draw `scaleX 0 to 1` with `transform-origin: left`, 350ms `power2.inOut`. Scale drama: a word steps by size jump (64px to 160px) as a hard cut on the beat or a 350ms `power3.inOut` scale from a left-top origin, never `back` or `elastic`. Exits are cuts or 250ms `power2.in` slides along a grid line. Hold 800-1500ms. Camera: locked-off; a pan is a translateX of `#world` that lands exactly on a column boundary (700ms `power2.inOut`). Der Film reading: stack 3 to 5 copies of one word at 0.2-0.6 opacity with offsets of 0-12px and blur 0-6px, stepped on every frame with `steps(1)`: montage by overprint.
3D (Rasan3D, no orthographic camera exists in the stage; fake it with a long lens): `camera: { pos: [[0,[0,0,40]]], target: [[0,[0,0,0]]], lens: [[0,135]] }` so perspective is nearly flat; flat planes via `k.pxPlane(group,{x,y,w,h,color})` with `k.material("unlit",{color:"#fc5b02"})`, type as `k.text({text, font, size, weight:700, color, align:"left"})` or `k.extrudeText` with `depth` 0.02 (thin slab) and `bevel` 0; no `k.rig`, no `k.ground` shadows, `environment: "none"`, `toneMapping: "none"`, `motionBlur` default. Tween planes along x or y only and stop on grid values; a `k.pass` at `"post"` is rarely needed here. DOM type pinned with `k.pinDom(el,{at:[x,y,0],face:"camera"})` stays crisp.

## How to instruct a model to build it
Paste: "Swiss International Style. 1920x1080, ground #e8e9dd (or black #000 for a night scene), ink #000, one flat accent #fc5b02, used once per scene. Use CSS grid: 8 columns x 4 rows, 24px gaps, 120px left margin, 60px other margins; draw the grid first as 1px lines and place every element by grid-column/grid-row. Type: Hanken Grotesk 700 display, Inter 400 body, flush-left ragged-right, only 16/32/64/160px, tracking -0.02em on display. No centred text, no gradients, no shadows, no rounded corners, no icons. Motion: line mask reveals 450ms power3.out with 70ms stagger, rules draw left to right 350ms power2.inOut, hard cuts on the beat, locked camera, 800-1500ms holds. Scale jumps are the drama." Claude tends to honour grid numbers literally; GPT-family models tend to add soft shadows and rounded cards, so state the bans explicitly (an observation from the earlier pass, not a measured result).

## Blending notes
Carries: grid, flush-left, scale jumps, one flat accent, hard cuts. Pairs with Bauhaus and Ulm/Braun (functionalism), Polish posters (Swiss structure under painterly panels) and Dutch Total Design. Breaks with Memphis, Emigre and layered noise; if forced, keep the grid and let only the accent behave differently.
