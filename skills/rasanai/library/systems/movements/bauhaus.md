## What it is
A German school of art, craft and design, 1919 to 1933 (Weimar, Dessau from 1925, Berlin), under Gropius, then Hannes Meyer, then Mies van der Rohe. It set out to unify craft, fine art and industrial production, and its graphic output (exhibition posters, postcards, letterheads, Bayer's printing and advertising workshop from 1925) turned the school's ideas into a look: geometric, asymmetric, bar-driven, lowercase, two or three flat colours. The first big public statement was the 1923 Weimar exhibition, with a Joost Schmidt poster that won an internal competition and doubled as a report to the state government that funded the school (germanhistorydocs.org). A series of artist postcards (Kandinsky, Klee, Schlemmer, Moholy-Nagy, Helm and others) for the same exhibition is the best fetchable record of the actual palette.

## The rules that make it this and not something else
- Function and construction first; no applied ornament. Every shape is a structural element of the layout.
- Primitive geometry (circle, square, triangle) and ruler-and-compass letterforms. Bayer's Universal (1925) reduced the alphabet to lowercase and "pure geometric forms that could be drawn with ruler and compass" (Letterform Archive); it was never cast as metal type, it served as a lettering model. The school's one released Bauhaus-master face, Bayer-Type, came in 1933.
- One alphabet: lowercase only. Bayer's reasoning was efficiency (speech does not distinguish capitals; one case speeds setting and reading) (encyclopedia.design).
- Modular stencil alphabets (Josef Albers, Joost Schmidt) built from rectangles, circles and semicircles on a grid; letters are assembled, not drawn.
- Bars and rules carry the page; type runs horizontal or vertical. Asymmetric balance; a diagonal as the single accent.
- Photomontage and unusual viewpoints (Moholy-Nagy "new vision"); in motion this reads as frames that tilt 45 degrees, not as drifting cameras.
- Colour is restrained in print: warm paper, black, and one or two spots. Kandinsky's yellow-red-blue / triangle-square-circle pairing was a classroom proposition, so treat it as an option, not as the law.

## Tokens decoded
- canvas #f8f0d5 (measured, cream paper), ink #413938 (measured, warm near-black), yellow #f7cf0d (measured), red #b5352a (proposed; the sampled cards were a muted brick #764542 / #a76769), blue #1f4e9c (proposed). In a scene use ink + canvas + at most two accents; the third colour appears only in the hero beat.
- Display: Jost 800 lowercase (OFL on Google Fonts, `wght` axis, tween 300 to 800 for a "build" feel) or League Spartan 700. Body Jost 400 at about 2.4% of the short side at 1080p (26px). Tracking 0 on display, +0.01em body; leading 0.95 display, 1.35 body.
- Bars 16px (1.5% of the short side), always touching an edge or another shape; circle diameter = a multiple of the bar width (proposed, makes the construction feel designed).
- Grid proposed: 12 columns, 8px baseline, 80px margins; place the headline on column 1 or 2 and leave at least 5 columns empty.
- No radius except true circles; no shadow; no gradient.

## Motion and camera
2D (HyperFrames, one paused GSAP timeline):
- Bar: `scaleX 0 -> 1`, `transformOrigin: "left center"`, 500ms `power2.out`. Never fade a bar in.
- Circle: `scale 0 -> 1` from centre, 450ms `power2.out` (no `back.out`; Bauhaus does not bounce). Square: `rotation -90 -> 0` plus `scale 0.6 -> 1`, 600ms `power2.inOut`.
- Type: line-mask reveal (`yPercent 100 -> 0` inside `overflow:hidden`) or a left clip `inset(0 100% 0 0) -> inset(0 0 0 0)` 400ms `power3.inOut`; stagger 80ms. All lowercase.
- Self-assembly: shapes from seeded offstage positions to layout positions, 0.4-0.6s each, `expo.out`, stagger 60ms outward from the largest shape.
- Section transition: the whole `#world` rotates 45 degrees while a colour field wipes (colour-field wipe, 0.35s `power3.inOut`); hold 1000ms after the last element lands. Camera tier T0 (locked), T1 push of 2% on the hold.
3D (Rasan3D): build with `k.THREE` primitives (BoxGeometry, SphereGeometry, ConeGeometry, CylinderGeometry) and `k.material("matte", { color })` in the three flat colours; `k.ground({ y: 0, color: "#f8f0d5" })` as the cream floor (a real floor, so the shadow reads as a drawn shadow). Light: `k.rig("low-key", { dir: [-0.5, 0.8, 0.6], shadows: true, shadowSoftness: 2 })` for one hard key (a hard shadow is the Bauhaus shadow; `top-soft` is too soft). Camera: a graphic, near-orthographic view by distance, not by projection: `lens` 135-200mm with `pos` far back, `fstop` unset so everything stays sharp; keys `pos: k.orbit({ center:[0,0.5,0], radius:14, height:9, from:35, to:55, t0:0, t1:6, ease:"power2.inOut" })`, then hold. Assemble solids in `pose(t,k)` from `k.rng(7)` scattered positions to the composition with `k.prog(t, a, b, "expo.out")`, all pure functions of t. Pin the lowercase headline with `k.pinDom(el, { at:[0,0,0], width:6, face:"camera" })` or keep it in the 2D `ui` layer. Tubular steel: `k.tube([[0,0,0],[0,1,0],[1,1,0],[1,0,0]], { radius:0.03, segments:80, radial:12 })` with `k.material("chrome")` only if the brief wants Breuer's chair; otherwise keep the world matte.

## How to instruct a model to build it
Paste: "Bauhaus, 1923 exhibition ephemera. Canvas #f8f0d5 (paper), ink #413938, accents red #b5352a and yellow #f7cf0d, blue #1f4e9c only as a single hero element; max two accents per scene. Only circles, squares, triangles and 16px bars that touch an edge. Headlines in Jost 800, lowercase only (never Title Case), left-aligned on column 1-2 of a 12-column grid, tied to a bar. Asymmetric composition; leave half the frame empty. Shapes assemble on screen: bars scaleX from the left (500ms power2.out), circles scale from centre (450ms power2.out, no overshoot), squares rotate 90 degrees into place (600ms power2.inOut). One 45 degree whole-frame rotation as the section change. No gradients, shadows, glass, rounded rectangles or italics." Claude and GPT both drift back to Title Case and to centred layouts; say "lowercase only" and "left-aligned, never centred" explicitly and check stills.

## Blending notes
Carries: primitives, lowercase, bars, flat cream-and-ink with one or two spots. Pairs with Swiss (cleaner grid, less colour), De Stijl (orthogonal only, primaries), constructivism (add diagonals, photomontage, red-black), Ulm/Braun (the functional descendant). Breaks with Art Nouveau (ornament), Memphis and postmodern (pattern, irony).
