## What it is
Mid-century corporate identity turned a logo into a programme: name, mark, colours, typefaces, grid and rules applied across every touchpoint (Wikipedia, Corporate identity: the concept entered business vocabulary around the 1940s). Paul Rand (IBM 1956, Westinghouse 1960, UPS 1961, ABC 1962, NeXT 1986) brought Swiss-influenced American Modernism to the corporation. Lester Beall (1903-1969) used bold primary colours with arrows and lines, as in the 1939 REA poster. Otl Aicher (Ulm School co-founder 1953) led Lufthansa (1962-63, with the crane in a circle, dominant yellow with dark blue accents, lowercase Helvetica Bold) and the Munich 1972 Olympics, whose pictograms influenced DOT symbols.

## The rules that make it this and not something else
- A mark that works "blurred or mutilated" and favours "utmost simplicity and restraint" (Rand, Wikipedia).
- IBM stripes (1972): half-tone bars lighten a heavy City-set mark; 8 bars as default, 13 for refined uses such as executive stationery; Rand said the problem was an uninterrupted jump from narrow to wide letters (inkbotdesign, search result).
- Colour is coded: Lufthansa yellow for speed, safety and freshness, blue for seriousness and efficiency (Disegno search result).
- Munich 72: bright blue and white as main colours with silver, light green, orange; no red, black, gold, brown or purple to avoid Nazi-era associations (otlaicher.de, Design is fine, search results).
- Pictograms on a strict grid with 45 and 90 degree angles (Wikipedia).
- Grid first: modular grids, left alignment, white space, sans-serif type (Swiss Style, Wikipedia).
- The system is a manual: usage rules, clear space, and consistent application are the product.

## Tokens decoded
- Beall measured (REA poster): paper `#f3eddf`, red `#d0222b`, blue `#2c56af`, ink `#514a46`: a corporate system can be warm paper plus two primaries. Proposed (unmeasured): white `#ffffff`, ink `#111111`, brand blue `#1f6fb6`, Lufthansa-style yellow `#ffad00`, Munich-style sky `#4fb3e6`, green `#7cc242`, orange `#ee7c1c`. Pick one or two per film. The otlaicher.de feature describes Munich 72 as a rainbow system (the "kinship approach": variable elements under one fixed concept), so a multi-colour system is legitimate if every colour has a fixed job.
- Type: Inter Tight 700 lowercase wordmark or Roboto Slab 700 caps for a City-like slab; body Inter 400; sizes 12 / 24 / 48 / 96 (a 2x scale).
- Logo clear space: 1x the height of the logo's main letter on all sides.
- IBM stripe: 8 bars, each bar height 1/8 of the letter height, gap 0.5 bar width (proportions proposed, unverified).
- Pictogram grid: 24 x 24 modules, 45/90 degree strokes only, 2-module stroke width.

## Motion and camera
2D (HyperFrames; timings proposed): the stripe wipe uses `steps(8)` over 600ms; if the stepped bars are rendered, set `data-finish-blur="off"` on that scene's frame root so the film finish does not average the steps. The wipe uses `steps(8)` over 600ms so bars arrive discretely; lock-up assembly: mark `scale` 0.96 -> 1 with opacity 0 -> 1 in 350ms `power3.out`; pictogram strokes draw on grid 200ms per stroke `power2.inOut`. Use the same enter on every scene; hold 1000ms. Camera: locked and orthogonal; push-in only 1.0 -> 1.04 over 5s.
3D (Rasan3D; real API, parameters proposed): the mark and its stripes as extruded signage. `k.svg(markSvg,{width:4,depth:0.12,bevel:0.01,material:k.material("matte",{color:"#1f6fb6"})})` for the mark (the stripe bars are separate paths so each can be keyed on `pose`: `bar.position.x = k.at(t,[[0.2+i*0.075,-3],[0.5+i*0.075,0,"steps(1)"]])` or a stepped `Math.round` of `k.prog`), or `k.extrudeText("ibm",{font:"assets/fonts/RobotoSlab.ttf",size:1.2,depth:0.15,material:k.material("matte",{color:"#1f6fb6"})})`. White cove: `k.ground({y:0,color:"#ffffff"})` plus `background:"#ffffff"`, `k.rig("top-soft",{key:"#ffffff",fill:"#f2f6ff",intensity:1,shadows:true,shadowSoftness:8})`, `environment:"soft"`. Camera `lens:[[0,50]]`, `pos` `Rasan3D.orbit({center:[0,0.6,0],radius:7,height:0.6,from:-3,to:3,t0:0,t1:6,ease:"power1.inOut"})` (6 degrees over 6s), no DoF. Pictograms: flat `k.pxPlane` planes on a 24-module grid so the 2D drawing and the 3D scene share one seam. A pinned manual page with `k.pinDom` shows clear-space guides.

## How to instruct a model to build it
"Corporate-identity scene on white. A lowercase wordmark in Inter Tight 700 in #1f6fb6, 96px, flush left on a 12-column grid. An 8-bar stripe mask wipes the wordmark in over 600ms with `steps(8)`. Then three pictograms, 24x24 module grids, 45/90 degree strokes, draw on one by one, 200ms each, `power2.inOut`. Every scene repeats the same enter move: `scale` 0.96 -> 1 and fade in 350ms `power3.out`. One accent colour at most. No gradient, shadow or glow. Show clear-space guides for 800ms as proof of the system." Claude: write the rules as a short manual then apply them. GPT: stress consistency across scenes and no decoration.

## Blending notes
Carries: grid, one family, coded colour, repeated move, stepped stripes, pictogram grid. Pairs with Swiss, Ulm and Japanese modern; warm hand-made systems need to stay out of the grid. Breaks if you swap the colour system per scene.
