# 3D: real space, built like a film

RasanAI films can mix 2D and 3D scene by scene, and inside one scene. A 3D scene is a real three.js world rendered by **Rasan3D** (`stage3d/rasan3d.js`, installed into a project as `assets/three/`) on the same paused GSAP timeline as the 2D, so a flat card can lift off the page into depth on the frame the cut lands, a camera can fly from one scene into the next, and a label can stay pinned to a point on a turning product. Every frame is a pure function of time, averaged over sub-frames for true motion blur, depth of field and anti-aliasing, and gated before it ships (`stage3d.mjs check`).

Opus is good at this: it can plan a camera, block a scene in world units, light it and land every move on its cue. The default it falls back to without a push is the three.js demo: a lone object spinning in a void under a flat light, the camera orbiting at constant speed. That's the film this file exists to beat. **Show off** (§11): plan the space, the light and the seams like a 3D lead at a top studio would, and make the 3D the moment people rewind.

Read this when the score (`motion/score.json`) gives a scene `space: "3d"` or `"hybrid"`, when the look is from the 3D & materials family, or when you're scoring a film (the Motion Director decides where 3D goes).

## 1. Where 3D goes

Every scene has a **space**: `2d` (DOM and GSAP, the default), `3d` (the Rasan3D world is the shot) or `hybrid` (a 3D layer between 2D ground and 2D type). Pick per scene, by what the scene has to show:

| Use 3D when the scene needs… | Example |
|---|---|
| an object to be **an object**: turned, opened, looked around, lit | the product, a device-less UI slab, the logo as a physical thing |
| **space** the story moves through | the camera travelling from the overview into one feature; a corridor of past versions |
| **layers in depth** | the UI exploded into its real layers (shell, panels, content) to show how it's built |
| a **2D thing becoming real** | the flat card from the last scene lifts off the page and turns (flat-to-depth) |
| **many things with one light** | a field of cards, a city of bars (data as architecture), a point cloud resolving into the product |
| **material** as the message | glass (clarity), chrome (precision), clay (friendly), concrete (solid) |

Keep it 2D when the scene is text to read, a dense UI flow the viewer must follow, a chart whose values matter, or anything where depth would only decorate. **One film, one space grammar:** decide how often depth appears (one 3D hero beat; a 3D spine the whole film travels through; 2D with 3D seams at the hinges) and keep to it. A film that flips space every scene without a reason reads as a tech demo.

Rules of thumb for the Motion Director:
- Films of 4 scenes or more get **at least one 3D or hybrid scene** unless the look forbids depth (a strictly flat style: Swiss, risograph, flat editorial) and the score says so in `depth.none_because`. Even flat styles can earn one: paper that folds in real space, a print sheet the camera pulls back from.
- The **signature seam or the reveal** is the strongest place for 3D: it's where the film turns, and space is the biggest change you can make.
- Prefer **2D ↔ 3D continuity** to cutting between unrelated worlds (§6).
- 3D scenes cost render time (§10). Two or three hero 3D shots beat eight average ones.

## 2. How a 3D scene is built

Three layers, back to front, all inside one HyperFrames sub-composition and all clocked by one paused timeline:

```
#r3-<frame>-ground   2D  the ground (a clip) and anything the 3D world flies over
#r3-<frame>-gl       3D  the Rasan3D canvas: transparent where nothing is drawn
#r3-<frame>-ui       2D  type, labels, callouts: crisp DOM, animated with GSAP as usual
```

Start from the template, never from a blank file:

```bash
node $SKILL_DIR/scripts/stage3d.mjs install  --project videos/<name>          # once per project (video.mjs inject does it too)
node $SKILL_DIR/scripts/stage3d.mjs scaffold --project videos/<name> --frame <frame_id> --duration <s> \
     --canvas "<frame.md canvas>" --ink "<ink>" --accent "<accent>" [--force]   # writes compositions/frames/<frame_id>.html
```

The scaffold is a valid HyperFrames frame (one `<template>`, styles and scripts inside it, `#root` with `data-composition-id`, `data-duration`, clips with timing, one paused timeline registered at `window.__timelines["<frame_id>"]`, element ids prefixed `r3-<frame_id>-` so they're unique and start with a letter). It already shows the flat-to-depth seam, a lit panel with a shadow on a catcher, DOM type over the 3D, and a 2D label pinned to a 3D point. Replace its world with the score's shots.

```html
<script src="https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/gsap@3.14.2/dist/gsap.min.js"></script>
<script src="assets/three/rasan3d.js"></script>          <!-- project-root relative, inside the <template> -->
<script>
  const tl = gsap.timeline({ paused: true });           // the clock: 2D tweens go on it as usual
  window.__timelines["04-reveal"] = tl;
  Rasan3D.stage({
    id: "04-reveal", canvas: document.getElementById("r3-04-reveal-gl"), timeline: tl, duration: 5.2,
    camera: { pos: [[0, [0, 0, 12]], [1.4, [3.2, 2.1, 7.4], "power3.inOut"]], target: [[0, [0, 0, 0]]], lens: [[0, 50]], fstop: 2.8 },
    async build(k) { /* make the world once; return the things pose() moves */ },
    pose(t, k) { /* set every animated property from t alone */ },
    onDraw(t, k) { /* optional: pin DOM to 3D from the same pose */ },
  });
</script>
```

What the runtime does for you: it adds one clock tween to `tl` (a plain object target, so the motion contract treats it as exempt) and draws on every seek; it holds HyperFrames' render until the scene is built (`window.__hf.buildReady`); it shares one WebGL renderer across every 3D scene in the film (ten 3D scenes, one GL context) and copies each frame into the scene's own canvas; it accumulates sub-frames across a shutter for motion blur, across the pixel for anti-aliasing and across a lens aperture for depth of field; and it tone-maps lit materials while leaving unlit UI faces colour-exact.

**Rules that keep it renderable** (the gate enforces them):
- `pose(t, k)` sets **every** animated property from `t` alone. No `+=`, counters, physics state or anything carried between calls: the renderer seeks out of order and renders sub-frames around each frame.
- Randomness only from `k.rng(seed)`. Never `Math.random()`, `Date.now()`, `performance.now()`, `requestAnimationFrame`, `setAnimationLoop`, or your own `WebGLRenderer`.
- Assets from the project (`assets/...`, project-root relative), loaded in `build()`. Never a URL at render time, never three.js from a CDN.
- Eases are GSAP names from `motion.md`, written on the keys. A key with no ease is an error; `none` (constant speed) is an error unless declared (`declare: { linear: ["<track>"] }`, for a conveyor or a clock hand, with the reason in your report).

## 3. API

### `Rasan3D.stage(options)`

| Option | Default | What |
|---|---|---|
| `id` | required | the composition id (the `window.__timelines` key) |
| `canvas` | required | the scene's canvas element (or a selector) |
| `timeline` | required | the scene's paused GSAP timeline: the clock |
| `duration` | `timeline.duration()` | seconds; set it (the timeline may be shorter than the scene) |
| `width`, `height`, `fps` | 1920, 1080, 30 | the composition's size and frame rate (fps sets the shutter) |
| `camera` | — | declarative camera keys (below). Use it: the gate checks its eases, and `layout()` reads it |
| `motionBlur` | `{ shutter: 0.5 }` | shutter as a fraction of the frame (0.5 = 180°); `samples: N` fixes the count; `false` turns it off |
| `antialias` | `true` | at least 4 jittered samples per frame in final quality |
| `environment` | `"studio"` | image-based light for reflections: `"studio"`, `"soft"`, `"none"`, or `{ preset, intensity }` (default intensity 0.5) |
| `background` | transparent | a colour makes the 3D layer opaque (needed under glass, §5) |
| `fog` | — | `{ color, near, far }`: atmospheric depth; colour it like the ground |
| `toneMapping`, `exposure` | `"neutral"`, 1 | lit materials only: `neutral` keeps brand colours, `agx` is filmic, `aces` punchy, `none` |
| `post` | `{}` | `bloom: { strength, threshold (1.15), knee, radius }`, `halation`, `grain` (0.015–0.04), `vignette`, `ca` (px), `saturation`, `lift: [r,g,b]`, `gain: [r,g,b]`. Post touches only what the 3D layer draws |
| `near`, `far`, `unitsPerMeter` | 0.05, 400, 1 | clip planes; world units per metre (for the f-stop) |
| `build(k)` | — | async; make the world once; return an object (it becomes `k.objects`) |
| `pose(t, k)` | — | place everything at local time `t` |
| `onDraw(t, k)` | — | after each frame: DOM pinned to 3D (`k.toScreen`) |
| `declare` | — | `{ linear: [track names], plain: true }`: exemptions the gate accepts (plain = a deliberately flat frame) |

### Camera keys

```js
camera: {
  pos:    [[t, [x, y, z]], [t, [x, y, z], "power3.inOut"], …],   // or a function of t: Rasan3D.orbit({...})
  target: [[t, [x, y, z]], …],     // where it looks
  lens:   [[t, 50], …],            // focal length in mm, full frame (36 mm on the frame's long side)
  roll:   [[t, 0], …],             // degrees
  focus:  [[t, "target"]],         // focus distance: "target" (default) or a number
  fstop:  2.8,                     // real depth of field (or aperture: <radius in world units>)
}
```

Keys are `[time, value, ease]`; the ease is the ease **arriving at** that key (like a GSAP `.to`); before the first key the value holds, after the last it holds. A key that repeats the previous value is a hold.

### The kit (`k`, handed to `build`, `pose`, `onDraw`)

| | |
|---|---|
| `k.THREE`, `k.scene`, `k.camera`, `k.stage`, `k.objects` | three.js r181 (the vendored copy), the scene, the camera, the stage, what `build` returned |
| `k.at(t, keys)`, `k.prog(t, a, b, ease)`, `k.ease(name)` | keyed values; 0–1 progress over `[a, b]`; a GSAP ease function. Use these in `pose` so the gate sees your eases |
| `k.lens(mm)`, `k.orbit({ center, radius, height, from, to, t0, t1, ease })` | mm → field of view; an eased orbit path (degrees) for `camera.pos` |
| `k.rng(seed)` | seeded random (mulberry32) |
| `k.material(kind, { color, …overrides })` | presets: `glass`, `frosted-glass`, `chrome`, `brushed-metal`, `clay`, `ceramic`, `gummy`, `plastic`, `concrete`, `paper`, `matte`, `emissive` (HDR, the only thing that blooms), `unlit` |
| `k.rig(kind, { key, fill, rim, dir, intensity, shadows, shadowSize, shadowSoftness })` | lighting rigs: `three-point`, `rim`, `top-soft`, `low-key`, `window`; `dir` is the key's direction `[x, y, z]` |
| `k.ground({ y, color, shadowOpacity, size })` | a shadow catcher (transparent, only the shadow; the 2D ground shows through) or, with `color`, a real floor |
| `k.image(url)` | a texture from a real screenshot or image (sRGB, exact) |
| `k.text({ text, font, size, weight, color, background, width, height, align, valign, lineHeight, letterSpacing, scale })` | type set in the document's own fonts as a texture (load the `@font-face` first) |
| `k.panel({ width, height, depth, radius, texture, bodyColor, inset })` | a UI slab: rounded, with real depth and bevel, the texture on its face, unlit so the UI stays exact |
| `k.svg(svgText, { width, depth, bevel, material })`, `k.svgUrl(url, …)` | the official logo (or any SVG) extruded; `material` may be a function of each path's colour |
| `k.extrudeText(text, { font (TTF/OTF), size, depth, bevel, align, material })` | real 3D type in the brand's font (TTF or OTF, not WOFF2) |
| `k.surfacePoints(mesh, count, seed)` | seeded points on a mesh's surface (point clouds, assembly from particles) |
| `k.layout({ at, distance })`, `k.pxPlane(group, { x, y, w, h, texture \| color })` | the 2D ↔ 3D seam (§6): a group in composition pixels on the plane the camera sees at time `at` |
| `k.toScreen(vec3)` | a world point → composition pixels `{x, y, visible}` at the current pose |
| `k.addon(path)` | three.js addons shipped with the runtime: `loaders/GLTFLoader.js`, `loaders/SVGLoader.js`, `loaders/TTFLoader.js`, `geometries/RoundedBoxGeometry.js`, `geometries/TextGeometry.js`, `math/MeshSurfaceSampler.js`, `utils/BufferGeometryUtils.js`, `environments/RoomEnvironment.js` |

A GLB model from the project: `const { GLTFLoader } = await k.addon("loaders/GLTFLoader.js"); const g = await new GLTFLoader().loadAsync("assets/models/x.glb");` (animated clips: an `AnimationMixer`, `mixer.setTime(t)` in `pose`).

## 4. Camera: plan it like a DP

**World units are metres.** Block the scene at real scale (a phone is 0.15, a laptop 0.3, a room 4) and the lens and f-stop behave like a real camera's. Put the subject near the origin, the floor at y = 0 or below it.

| Lens | Feel | Use |
|---|---|---|
| 14–24 mm | exaggerated space, strong perspective, near things huge | flying through a world, architecture, the inside of something |
| 35 mm | natural, documentary | moving through a space with the viewer |
| 50 mm | neutral, honest | the default; UI slabs, objects at arm's length |
| 85–135 mm | compressed, flattered, background melts | product hero, the reveal, type in space |
| 200 mm+ | flat, graphic, stacked layers | a row of objects compressed into a pattern; a calm, monumental hold |

| Move | Build it | Feel |
|---|---|---|
| push-in (dolly) | `pos` toward `target`, lens fixed | intimacy, attention; near things grow faster than far (parallax) |
| zoom | `lens` changes, `pos` fixed | flat, graphic, a "look closer" |
| dolly zoom | `pos` back while `lens` lengthens (or the reverse), subject size held | vertigo, realisation; once per film at most |
| truck / track | `pos` and `target` slide sideways together | following, revealing a row |
| crane / pedestal | `pos.y` rises or falls with `target` | reveal of scale, an arrival |
| orbit / arc | `pos: k.orbit({…})`, 15–60° over the shot, eased | understanding an object; never a full constant-speed spin |
| rack focus | `focus` keys from one distance to another, fstop 1.8–2.8 | shifting attention between two planes |
| whip | a fast `target` swing (0.15–0.3 s, `expo.inOut`) with motion blur | a hard change of subject; cut at the blur's peak |
| fly-through | `pos` through an opening along a path, wide lens | going inside: the transition into the next scene |

- **One move per shot**, motivated (toward what matters, away to reveal context, with an action). Hold before and after: land, then let it be read. A camera that never stops is the screensaver (the gate warns `never-rests`).
- **Ease every leg**: `power3.inOut` or the film's `move` ease for repositioning, `expo.out` or `power4.out` for a slam that lands, `sine.inOut` for slow, luxurious drift. Never `none`.
- **Lead the subject**: the camera arrives 0.1–0.2 s after the subject's own move starts, never at the same instant.
- **Camera tiers still apply** (`craft.md` §6): T0 locked, T1 lean-in (a 2–5% push), T2 focus move into a region, T3 the one crash move.
- Motion blur is on by default (180° shutter). Fast camera moves read as speed, not strobing; a still frame costs 4 samples.
- Depth of field: f/2–2.8 for a product hero or a rack focus, f/4–5.6 for a readable UI slab, off for text-heavy frames.

## 5. Light and materials

**One motivated key**, a direction and a colour, and everything agrees with it: the rig's `dir`, the shadow, the rim, any 2D shadows on the same scene. Choose the rig by mood:

| Rig | Look | For |
|---|---|---|
| `three-point` | key, soft fill, back rim | the neutral default; products, UI slabs |
| `top-soft` | a big soft top light, gentle wrap | products on a sweep; clean, Apple-like |
| `rim` | dark, sculpted by back light | dramatic reveals, chrome, type in the dark |
| `low-key` | one hard key, deep shadow | tension, the problem beat, cinematic |
| `window` | broad side light | warm, human, editorial |

Materials by the look's 3D family (the style's palette gives the colours; the accent is the one emissive or saturated thing):

| Style | Material | Light | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Glass 3D | `glass` / `frosted-glass` | `rim` or `three-point`, `environment: "studio"` | glass refracts only what the 3D layer draws: give it an opaque `background` or a backdrop plane, never bare DOM behind it |
| Chrome / liquid metal | `chrome` | `rim`, environment 0.8–1.2 | metal is its reflections: it needs the environment and something to reflect |
| Matte ceramic, soft 3D | `ceramic` / `clay` | `top-soft`, soft shadows (`shadowSoftness` 8–12) | low saturation, big radii, slow moves |
| Gummy / gel | `gummy` | `three-point` with a warm rim | `background` set; light passes through |
| Concrete brutalist | `concrete` | `low-key` or `window`, hard shadows | monumental lens (85 mm+), slow crane |
| Point cloud | points from `k.surfacePoints` (a `Points` with an `unlit`/emissive look) | none needed | resolve the cloud into the product on the beat |
| Paper / editorial | `paper`, planes with print textures | `window` | real folds and curls; type stays DOM |
| Product UI | `panel` with the real screenshot (`k.image`) | `three-point` | the face is unlit: the UI keeps its exact colours |

Bloom touches only HDR light (`emissive` at intensity 2–4). UI white and lit surfaces stay below the threshold on purpose: glow on everything is slop.

## 6. 2D ↔ 3D seams (where the planning shows)

The cut between a 2D scene and a 3D one is where this pays off or falls apart. Plan these seams in the score (`kind` below, with handoff numbers in px like any continuity seam) and build them exactly:

| Seam | What happens | Build |
|---|---|---|
| `flat-to-depth` | the last frame of a 2D scene **is** the first frame of the 3D one; then the flat thing lifts into space | in `build`: `const g = k.layout({ at: 0, distance: D })`; put the object (a `panel`, a `pxPlane` with the same screenshot) at the 2D element's exact px rect in `g` (`g.userData.pxPerUnit` converts px to world). The camera's t = 0 key is the layout pose; move it after a short hold (0.1–0.3 s) |
| `depth-to-flat` | the 3D move lands on a pose where an object fills an exact px rect; the next 2D scene starts there | lay the object out with `k.layout({ at: <end time> })`; the camera's last key is that pose; hold 2+ frames before the cut |
| `camera-through` | one continuous camera move crosses the cut: the camera flies into something in scene N (a screen, a window, the O of a word) and scene N+1 opens inside it | velocity-match: scene N's last camera leg and N+1's first leg share direction and speed (write both in the seam's `out` / `in`: `speed` in px/s of the subject or world units/s, `direction`); cut at peak speed under motion blur |
| `shared-element` (2D ↔ 3D) | a DOM element and a 3D object are the same thing across the cut (a button becomes a slab, a logo extrudes) | the 3D object is laid out at the DOM element's px rect at the seam time (`layout`); its colours match exactly (unlit face, same texture) |
| `pinned` (inside a hybrid scene) | 2D labels, cursors and leader lines ride on 3D points | `onDraw`: `const p = k.toScreen(point)`, set the DOM element's transform; hide it when `!p.visible` |

Seam discipline: the frames on both sides match to within a pixel and a colour level (look at a strip across the cut at 15 fps). An exact match is what makes the viewer believe the flat thing and the 3D thing are one object, and it's what they rewind to see.

## 7. Type and UI in 3D

- **Readable type stays DOM** in the `ui` layer above the 3D, set from `frame.md`: crisp at every size, animated with the film's text techniques, checked by the motion contract.
- **Display type can be 3D** when its being an object is the point: `k.extrudeText` in the brand's TTF/OTF (a hero word, a number, the end card), or `k.text` on a plane for type that sits in the world (a sign, a label on a surface). Nothing under ~48 px on screen in 3D.
- **The product's UI in 3D** is the real screenshot on a `panel` (never a mock): one slab for the screen, or exploded layers (shell, sidebar, content, the focused component) at z offsets 0.05–0.3 that separate on the beat and close back. The cursor stays DOM, pinned to the 3D point it clicks (`toScreen`).
- **The logo** is the official SVG through `k.svg` / `k.svgUrl`: never redrawn, never stretched; extrude depth 2–6% of its width.

## 8. Motion in 3D

The animation principles hold in depth: one primary mover per beat; secondaries 80–200 ms behind; arcs; overlap and follow-through (a slab lands, its layers settle 2–4 frames later); anticipation in the style's register. 3D adds:
- **Rotations land**: turn an object to a composed angle and hold it; 15–40° reads as "look at this", 180° as "the other side". No idle spin.
- **Scale the move to the lens**: the same 1 m of travel is violent on 24 mm and calm on 135 mm.
- **Parallax is the proof of depth**: put something near the lens (a blurred foreground slab, a row of cards) when the camera moves, or the 3D reads flat.
- **Assembly**: pieces fly in from seeded positions to their layout, staggered outward from the origin, each on `expo.out`; the last lands in the back half of the shot.

## 9. Seeing and checking

```bash
node $SKILL_DIR/scripts/stage3d.mjs stills --file <frame.html> --at 0,1.4,3.2 --out <dir>           # PNGs, final quality
node $SKILL_DIR/scripts/crew.mjs strip --file <frame.html> --from 0.8 --to 2 --fps 15 --out <sheet.png>  # the move, frame by frame
node $SKILL_DIR/scripts/stage3d.mjs check --project videos/<name> [--file <frame.html>] [--json]     # the 3D gate
```

Strips of a 3D scene wait for the build and show the real motion blur. Key frames drawn in 3D (`frames/<n>.html`, from `stage3d.mjs scaffold --standalone --out $RUN/frames/<n>.html`, with the runtime installed by `stage3d.mjs install --dest "$RUN/frames"`) render with `design.mjs stills` as usual: it waits for the scene.

`stage3d.mjs check` (exit 0 clean, 2 violations, 1 could not run):

| Rule | Severity | What |
|---|---|---|
| `build-failed`, `console-error` | error / warning | the scene didn't build, or the page threw |
| `not-seek-safe` | error | the same time rendered different pixels after seeking elsewhere (state carried between frames) |
| `blank-frame` | error | the 3D layer is a flat field (camera aimed at nothing, unlit, behind the near plane) |
| `implicit-ease`, `ease-outside-set`, `linear-drift` | error | a 3D key without an ease, an ease outside `motion.md`, a constant-speed move |
| `custom-ease` | warning | an ease function (can't be checked) |
| `fast-without-blur` | warning | travel over 6 px per frame with motion blur off |
| `never-rests` | warning | the world never comes to rest |
| `frame-cost` | warning | a final frame over 2.5 s (with the estimated render time) |
| `nondeterministic-random`, `wall-clock`, `free-running-loop`, `own-renderer`, `interactive-controls`, `placeholder-material`, `remote-three`, `remote-asset`, `runtime-path`, `runtime-missing` | error | code that breaks determinism, the shared renderer or offline rendering |
| `stock-primitive` | warning | a torus knot or icosahedron: the three.js demo tell |

`obey.mjs` still checks every GSAP tween in the scene's 2D layers; the 3D gate checks the 3D. Both run before the render.

## 10. Cost

On a Mac with a GPU (HyperFrames renders with the hardware GPU when it can), a hybrid scene renders in about real time to a few times real time. Under software GL (no GPU) a final frame with 16–24 samples, shadows and glass can take seconds. Keep it in budget:
- transmission (glass, gummy) on the hero only; everything else opaque
- one shadow-casting light (the key); fill and rim without shadows
- merge static geometry; reuse materials; textures at the size they appear
- `motionBlur.samples` lower (8) on long moves that are mostly calm; let `auto` decide otherwise
- check `frame-cost` in the gate, and look at `frame_ms` in its report

## 11. Show off in 3D

The bar is a 3D lead at Buck, ManvsMachine or Apple's product-film team. The moves that show what planning can do (pick the ones that serve the story; one done exactly beats three done roughly):

- **The flat thing becomes real.** The 2D card the viewer has been reading lifts off the page on the downbeat, turns 30° to show it has depth, and the next idea is on its back.
- **One camera, two scenes.** The camera flies into the product's screen and the next scene is inside it, velocity-matched, under motion blur, no cut visible.
- **The exploded view.** The real UI separates into its real layers in depth, the camera arcs 25° to show them, the one layer that matters lights, and they close back as the line lands.
- **Type as architecture.** The headline's letters stand as objects in space; the camera travels between them; the last word is the end card.
- **Data in space.** The numbers become a field of columns under one light; a rack focus moves from the old value to the new.
- **The logo, physically.** The official mark extruded in the brand's material, lit by one key, landing on the music's last hit with its shadow settling a frame later.
- **A dolly zoom on the realisation**, once, timed to the voiceover word.

And the craft that makes any of them land: light that agrees with itself, a lens chosen for a reason, every move eased and landed, motion blur on the fast frames, a pixel-exact seam, a hold long enough to read. **Never**: a spinning hero in a void, a constant-speed orbit, a torus knot, rainbow normals, a neon grid floor, a particle nebula, lens-flare soup, every object glowing, the camera drifting because it can.
